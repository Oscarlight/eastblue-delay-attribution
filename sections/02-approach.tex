\section{Problem Setting and Proposed Approach}
\label{sec:approach}

A click $i$ occurs at time $C_i$ with features $x_i$ and, if it converts, does so after a
delay $D_i$. The attribution window $v$ fixes the target $\Yv_i=\mathbf{1}[D_i\le v]$, knowable only at $C_i+v$. The join
delay $o$ fixes when the record is written, so the label available
at that moment is
\begin{equation}
  \Yo_i \;=\; \mathbf{1}\!\left[D_i \le o\right] \;\le\; \Yv_i .
  \label{eq:labels}
\end{equation}
Setting $o=v$ reproduces the conventional pipeline. Setting $o\ll v$ is what we want, so that
fast and slow conversions can share one training pipeline.

\paragraph{What a deployable pipeline must satisfy.}
At no moment can we train on conversions that have not yet landed, which is a straightforward
\textbf{causality rule}. In addition, we have the \textbf{single-visit} constraint: let $n_i$ be the number
of times a click $i$ enters the served model's loss over its lifetime. This constraint, widely used in production systems, requires
$\max_i n_i\le 1$.

\paragraph{Which published methods comply.}
We reproduced \nScreened{} published methods from scratch under a single protocol and counted
the records each one's served model consumes per distinct click. Of these, \nFail{} write
more than one.

\paragraph{Proposed methods.}
% What remains is a choice of what the single frozen target should contain. We study four answers,
% sharing a pipeline, schedule, feature set and optimiser, and differing only in the target
% $\tilde y_i$ and in the age $\Delta$ at which the record is written. 
We study four ways of generating the pipeline's training target. 
\textsc{Fresh} writes at
$\Delta=o$ with $\tilde y_i=\Yo_i$, applying no correction at all. \textsc{Wait} writes at
$\Delta=v$ with $\tilde y_i=\Yv_i$, which is the conventional method and our comparison baseline.
\textsc{Correct-Distill} writes at $\Delta=o$, keeps the observed
labels and softens only the observed negatives with a teacher model's prediction.
\begin{equation}
  \tilde y_i \;=\; \Yo_i + (1-\Yo_i)\,\wt(x_i),
  \qquad
  \wt(x) \approx P\!\left(\Yv{=}1 \,\middle|\, \Yo{=}0,\, x\right).
  \label{eq:hybrid}
\end{equation}
An observed positive stays exactly $1$, and only the uncertain mass moves. This is the functional
form used by ULC~\cite{wang2023unbiased}; the two differ in how $\wt$ is estimated. 
\textsc{Distill-Only} goes further still, replacing every observed label with the teacher's
prediction. The teacher model used by both \textsc{Distill-Only} and \textsc{Correct-Distill}
is itself trained with the conventional \textsc{Wait} method.
Finally, \textsc{Oracle} writes at $\Delta=o$ but labels the record
$\Yv_i$. It violates the causality rule and is not deployable. We include it because it bounds what any
causal correction can recover.
