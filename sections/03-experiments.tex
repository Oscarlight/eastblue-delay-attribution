\subsection{Experimental Setup}

\paragraph{Data and protocol.} We use the public Criteo conversion logs released
with~\cite{chapelle2014modeling}, the only large public advertising corpus carrying per-click
conversion timestamps. Clicks span 60 days. Days $[0,30)$ initialise the model causally and days
$[30,60)$ form the streaming period, divided into \nCohorts{} hourly rounds yielding \nEval{}
scored clicks at a base conversion rate of \baseRate{}. Learning rates are selected per arm and
per cell from a seven-point grid on a held-out slice of the initialisation period, before any
streaming evaluation, then pinned across seeds. The teacher's rate is fixed and decoupled from
the student's, so the student's sweep tunes one model rather than two.

\paragraph{Model architecture.} Every arm drives the same production-shaped backbone, so a
method never sees a different model from its comparators. The eight integer fields enter a dense
arm as $\log(1+x)$ paired with an observed-mask bit, normalised and passed through a two-layer
perceptron to a projection vector. The nine categorical fields are hashed per field and
embedded, one identifier per field, giving a token per field. Tokens and the dense projection

are then consumed by a stack of Wukong interaction layers, each of which
takes a token set and returns a token set of the same width so layers compose, combining a
factorisation-machine block for explicit pairwise interaction with a linear compression block
carried as a residual. The final token set is flattened to a single logit. Auxiliary networks
that a baseline needs, such as a delay head or an importance-weight classifier, are held at a
fixed size and never scaled with the served model, so the capacity axis measures the served
model alone.

\paragraph{Scaling the served model.} The five rungs $\mathrm{M}_0$ to $\mathrm{M}_4$ move three
dials together rather than one: the interaction trunk is taken from successive rungs of a
compute-efficient frontier, the hash width per categorical field grows $36 \to 256 \to 1{,}280
\to 16{,}384 \to 131{,}072$, and the embedding dimension widens $4 \to 4 \to 8 \to 8 \to 8$.
Scaling only table rows against a fixed trunk would make the axis a feature-resolution axis
rather than a capacity one, since on this corpus the trunk dominates the parameter count until
the tables are large. The result is roughly an order of magnitude per step, \capMin{} to
\capMax{} parameters and \flopMin{} to \flopMax{} MFLOPs per inference. Parameters and compute
do not track each other: $\mathrm{M}_0$ and $\mathrm{M}_1$ differ 5.2-fold in parameters at
identical compute because they share a trunk rung and differ only in table rows. We therefore
report both rather than let one stand for the other.

\paragraph{Axes.} Two attribution windows, $v=1$\,d and $v=7$\,d, stand for a fast and a slow
funnel; the contrast between them is the paper's point, so we never average across them. Three
join delays are used, $o\in\{5,30,90\}$ minutes, crossed with the five served capacities. The
fourth axis is the teacher, for which we study two architectures. \emph{Teacher 1} places
8-dimensional embedding tables under a two-layer perceptron, giving \teacherSmallParams{}
parameters at \teacherSmallFlops{} MFLOPs. \emph{Teacher 2} places 48-dimensional tables under a
deep interaction trunk, giving \teacherParams{} parameters at \teacherFlops{} MFLOPs. Running
both is what lets us report the method's sensitivity to the choice of teacher rather than to one
arbitrary configuration of it.

\paragraph{Scale and comparability.} The evidence comprises \nRunsBoth{} runs in two campaigns:
\nRunsThree{} runs over \nArmsThree{} arms reproducing the published methods under teacher~1, and \nRunsFour{} runs over \nArmsFour{} arms adding teacher~2, with
\seedsCore{} seeds on the headline arms and \seedsOther{} elsewhere. The second campaign runs a
frozen copy of the first's harness and re-executes \nShared{} of the first's runs, spanning
\nSharedArms{} arms, under its own source revision. Every one returns the identical $\NE$,
\textsc{Wait} included across all \nSharedWait{} of its runs. That measured reproduction, rather
than an assumption that two campaigns are comparable, is what licenses reading an arm from
either campaign as a gain against \textsc{Wait} on one axis.

\paragraph{Metric and statistics.} We report normalised entropy against $\Yv$, lower being
better, pooled over all scored clicks rather than averaged over rounds, since a per-round
average would normalise each hour by its own base rate and discard diurnal variation a model can
legitimately predict. \textsc{Wait} itself attains $\NE$ between \waitNeMin{} and \waitNeMax{}
across the grid, which is the base the relative figures below are read against. Results are the
relative reduction against \textsc{Wait} within a cell,
computed per seed and then averaged, so a positive number means the short join delay is better
than waiting for the matured label. Seeds differ only in initialisation and minibatch order, so
seed $i$ of two arms forms a matched pair: we use a paired $t$-test within each cell and
Holm--Bonferroni correction across the \nCells{} cells of a contrast, counting only cells whose
gain is positive and survives. Seed dispersion is small away from \textsc{Fresh}, with median
$\NE$ standard deviation \seedSdMed{} and maximum \seedSdMax{}. \textsc{Fresh} reaches
\seedSdFresh{} at $o=5$ minutes, a finding rather than a nuisance: an uncorrected censored
target at the shortest join delay is not a stable estimator. Seeds measure initialisation
variance on one data origin, establishing precision rather than generalisation.

\paragraph{A short join delay beats waiting, and by more on slow funnels.}
Writing the record \ratioSeven{} times sooner than the seven-day window it answers to, and
letting the teacher stand in for the conversions not yet seen, reduces $\NE$ in all \nCells{}
seven-day cells, of which \cdSevenSig{} survive Holm--Bonferroni correction. The reduction
ranges from \cdSevenMinAbs{}\% to \cdSevenMaxAbs{}\%, with a mean of \cdSevenMeanAbs{}\%. At the
one-day window the same substitution gains \cdOneMeanAbs{}\% on average, positive in
\cdOnePos{} of \nCells{} cells and significant in \cdOneSig{}. The two windows differ by a factor
of three, and the direction is the informative part. The slower the funnel, the more a short
join delay is worth. This inverts the natural expectation, since a longer window censors more of
the label and leaves the teacher more to supply. What dominates instead is the cost of the
alternative: \textsc{Wait} at $v=7$\,d trains on week-old data, and no amount of label accuracy
compensates for that. Both ends of the achievable interval are measured. \textsc{Oracle}, which
writes at $o$ but labels the record $\Yv$, gains \oracleSevenAbs{}\% and \oracleOneAbs{}\%, so
\textsc{Correct-Distill} recovers \recoverSeven{}\% and \recoverOne{}\% of what any causal
correction could attain. \textsc{Fresh}, the same early write with no correction at all, loses
\freshSevenAbs{}\% and \freshOneAbs{}\%. The deficit the teacher fills is an order of magnitude
larger than the gains above it.

\paragraph{At small capacity the hard label can be dropped entirely.}
The result above uses the hybrid target of Eq.~\eqref{eq:hybrid}. The fully soft target of
\textsc{Distill-Only}, which carries no observed label at all, shows how far the idea can be
pushed. At the two smallest served capacities it beats \textsc{Wait} in all \doSmallBeat{} of
\doSmallN{} cells, by \doOneSmallAbs{}\% at $v=1$\,d and \doSevenSmallAbs{}\% at $v=7$\,d. That
regime is the one retrieval and other early-funnel stages operate in, and the target suits it:
the record reduces to features and a single number, emission needs no join against the observed
outcome, and the pipeline still gains over waiting.

The target does not carry to the larger rungs. At $v=1$\,d it loses to \textsc{Wait} by
\doOneLargeAbs{}\% across the three largest capacities, crossing over between $\mathrm{M}_1$ and $\mathrm{M}_2$ at every
join delay as Fig.~\ref{fig:gain} shows, and at $v=7$\,d it stays ahead by only
\doSevenLargeAbs{}\%. A purely soft target plausibly acts as a regulariser, supplying a variance
reduction that a small model can use and a larger one no longer
needs~\cite{menon2021statistical}. Where capacity is ample, retaining the observed positive is
what pays: \textsc{Correct-Distill} beats \textsc{Distill-Only} in all
\cdBeatDo{} cells, and \textsc{Wait} in \cdBeatWait{} of \nCellsBoth{}. The \cdNegN{} exceptions
both occur at $v=1$\,d and $o=5$ minutes, at rungs \cdNegWhere{}, the worse by
\cdNegWorstAbs{}\%, and neither survives correction. Their location is coherent rather than
random: the shortest join delay at the fast window is where the teacher has least to add, since
\foOneFive{} of the conversions are already visible.

Four things follow. First, the one-record rule is cheap but not free: the best method overall is
\textsc{\sliceBest} at \sliceBestGain{}\%, and it buys its \slicePrice{}\,pp lead over us by
writing \vanillaDup{} records per click, which this pipeline cannot do. Second, among methods
that can actually run on such a stream ours is first, at
\sliceOurGain{}\,$\pm$\,\sliceOurCi{}\%, though its interval overlaps
\textsc{Ulc}~\cite{wang2023unbiased} at \sliceUlcGain{}\,$\pm$\,\sliceUlcCi{}\%, so at this cell
the two are level and the case for an offline teacher over an online corrector rests on
architecture rather than accuracy, as Section~\ref{sec:conclusion} argues. Over the full grid
the margin is larger, \ourUlcSeven{}\,pp at seven days across \ourUlcWinSeven{} of \nCells{}
cells. Third, \textsc{Oracle} bounds only the methods that obey Rule~2: at
\sliceOracleGain{}\% it is itself beaten by \sliceOverOracle{} arms that duplicate records,
because writing a click several times supplies training signal that no single-visit target can
recover, however perfect its label. Fourth, the gain does not come at the cost of calibration.
\textsc{Correct-Distill} reaches calibration error \sliceOurCal{} against \textsc{Wait}'s
\sliceWaitCal{} while improving $\NE$, whereas \textsc{Fresh}, the same early write left
uncorrected, degrades it to \sliceFreshCal{}, a factor of \sliceFreshCalRatio{}. Softening the
observed negatives repairs the censored target's bias, not merely its ranking.

\paragraph{Robustness to the teacher, and what bounds it on this corpus.}
Substituting teacher 1 for teacher 2 changes
little. \textsc{Correct-Distill} gains \tchSmallCdSevenAbs{}\% with teacher 1 against
\tchLargeCdSevenAbs{}\% with teacher 2 at seven days, and \tchSmallCdOneAbs{}\% against
\tchLargeCdOneAbs{}\% at one; over all \nCellsBoth{} cells the two architectures differ by
\teachCdMean{}\,pp for \textsc{Correct-Distill} and \teachDoMean{}\,pp for
\textsc{Distill-Only}. Holding parameters fixed while spending \wideFlopRatio{} times more
teacher compute stays inside the same margin. The teacher's learning rate is verified rather
than inherited: the default beats a rate ten times smaller in every cell tested and matches one
twice as large, so the insensitivity describes a converged teacher rather than an untuned one.
The practical reading is that the method does not depend on a particular teacher, which is what
makes it portable across pipelines.

That insensitivity is a property of the corpus as much as of the method. Criteo carries eight
integer and nine hashed categorical fields with no creative, content or user-history signal, so
$P(\Yv{=}1\mid\Yo{=}0,x)$ is close to exhausted by a modest architecture and further teacher
capacity has little left to condition on. A production corrector would see far richer features
and longer histories, and a cross-objective foundation model trained on fully matured
outcomes~\cite{meta2025gem} is precisely the artefact positioned to supply it. We expect such a
teacher to sharpen the corrector and widen the gains reported here, but that is an expectation
about a setting we did not measure, and we claim only the Criteo result.

Finally, fixing the teacher after causal initialisation, so that it is shipped as a static model
rather than maintained as a second training job, costs \frzCostOneAbs{} and
\frzCostSevenAbs{}\,pp, and the fixed-teacher arm still beats \textsc{Wait} in \frzBeat{} of
\frzN{} cells. Adoption therefore does not require standing up a continuously retrained
auxiliary alongside the served model.
