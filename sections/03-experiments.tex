\paragraph{Data and protocol.} We use the public Criteo conversion logs released
with~\cite{chapelle2014modeling}. Clicks span 60 days. Days $[0,30)$ initialise the model causally and days
$[30,60)$ form the streaming period, divided into \nCohorts{} hourly rounds yielding \nEval{}
scored clicks at a base conversion rate of \baseRate{}. Learning rates are selected per arm and
per cell from a seven-point grid on a held-out slice of the initialisation period, before any
streaming evaluation, then pinned across seeds. The teacher's rate is fixed and decoupled from
the student's.

arm as $\log(1+x)$ paired with an observed-mask bit, normalised and passed through a two-layer
perceptron to a projection vector. The nine categorical fields are hashed per field and
embedded, one identifier per field, giving a token per field. Tokens and the dense projection

\paragraph{Different setups and evaluation metric.} Two attribution windows ($v\in\{1, 7\}$ days), three
join delays ($o\in\{5,30,90\}$ minutes), serving models with four different complexities
($\mathrm{M}_0$ to $\mathrm{M}_3$), and two different teacher models are used. Across the four
complexities the hash width per categorical field grows $36 \to 256 \to 1{,}280 \to 16{,}384$
and the embedding dimension widens $4 \to 4 \to 8 \to 8$, roughly an order of magnitude of
parameters per step, \capMin{} to \capMax{} parameters and \flopMin{} to \flopMax{} MFLOPs per
inference. A fifth rung at full table resolution was run but is excluded here: it is the only
one built on the full trunk rather than a frontier node, so it costs 30 times the compute of
$\mathrm{M}_3$ for 8 times the parameters and breaks the ladder's spacing.
For the two teacher models, \emph{Teacher 1} places
8-dimensional embedding tables under a two-layer perceptron, giving \teacherSmallParams{}
parameters at \teacherSmallFlops{} MFLOPs. \emph{Teacher 2} places 48-dimensional tables under a
deep interaction trunk, giving \teacherParams{} parameters at \teacherFlops{} MFLOPs. Running
both is what lets us report the method's sensitivity to the choice of teacher rather than to one
arbitrary configuration of it. For each step, we ran five to ten seeds for statistic analysis.
We report normalised entropy and calibration against the matured $\Yv$ for all methods.

\paragraph{A short join delay beats waiting, and by more on slow funnels.}
Writing the record \ratioSeven{} times sooner than the seven-day window it answers to reduces
$\NE$ in all \nCells{} seven-day cells, \cdSevenSig{} of them surviving Holm--Bonferroni
correction, ranging from \cdSevenMinAbs{}\% to \cdSevenMaxAbs{}\% with a mean of
\cdSevenMeanAbs{}\%. At one day the same substitution gains \cdOneMeanAbs{}\%, positive in
\cdOnePos{} of \nCells{} cells and significant in \cdOneSig{}. The direction is the informative
part: the slower the funnel, the more a short join delay is worth, because \textsc{Wait} at
$v=7$\,d trains on week-old data and no amount of label accuracy compensates for that. Both ends
of the interval are measured. \textsc{Oracle} gains \oracleSevenAbs{}\% and \oracleOneAbs{}\%,
so \textsc{Correct-Distill} recovers \recoverSeven{}\% and \recoverOne{}\% of what any causal
correction could attain, while \textsc{Fresh} loses \freshSevenAbs{}\% and \freshOneAbs{}\%. The
deficit the teacher fills is an order of magnitude larger than the gains above it.

\paragraph{At small capacity the hard label can be dropped entirely.}
\textsc{Distill-Only} carries no observed label at all. At the two smallest capacities it beats
\textsc{Wait} in all \doSmallBeat{} of \doSmallN{} cells, by \doOneSmallAbs{}\% at $v=1$\,d and
\doSevenSmallAbs{}\% at $v=7$\,d, which suits retrieval and other early-funnel stages: the record
reduces to features and one number, and emission needs no join against the outcome. It does not
carry to the larger rungs, losing \doOneLargeAbs{}\% at $v=1$\,d and \doSevenLargeAbs{}\% at
$v=7$\,d across the two largest capacities, crossing over between
$\mathrm{M}_1$ and $\mathrm{M}_2$ at every join delay, plausibly because a soft target acts as a
regulariser that a larger model no longer needs~\cite{menon2021statistical}. Where capacity is
ample the observed positive is what pays: \textsc{Correct-Distill} beats \textsc{Distill-Only}
in all \cdBeatDo{} cells and \textsc{Wait} in \cdBeatWait{} of \nCellsBoth{}. The single
exception is at $v=1$\,d, $o=5$ minutes and rung \cdNegWhere{}, by \cdNegWorstAbs{}\%, and it
does not survive correction; that is where the teacher has least to add, since \foOneFive{} of
the conversions are already visible.

is worth \slSmallOur{}\,$\pm$\,\slSmallOurCi{}\% and takes \slSmallRecover{}\% of the

\paragraph{Robustness to the teacher.}
Substituting teacher 1 for teacher 2 changes little. \textsc{Correct-Distill} gains
\tchSmallCdSevenAbs{}\% against \tchLargeCdSevenAbs{}\% at seven days and \tchSmallCdOneAbs{}\%
against \tchLargeCdOneAbs{}\% at one, and over all \nCellsBoth{} cells the two differ by
\teachCdMean{}\,pp for \textsc{Correct-Distill} and \teachDoMean{}\,pp for
\textsc{Distill-Only}. Spending \wideFlopRatio{} times more teacher compute at fixed parameters
stays inside the same margin, and the default teacher learning rate beats one ten times smaller
in every cell tested, so this describes a converged teacher rather than an untuned one. Freezing
the teacher after initialisation, so that it ships as a static file rather than a second
training job, costs \frzCostOneAbs{} and \frzCostSevenAbs{}\,pp and still beats \textsc{Wait} in
\frzBeat{} of \frzN{} cells. The method therefore does not depend on a particular teacher, nor
on keeping one running. That insensitivity is partly a property of the corpus: Criteo carries
eight integer and nine hashed categorical fields with no creative, content or user-history
signal, so $P(\Yv{=}1\mid\Yo{=}0,x)$ is close to exhausted by a modest architecture. A
production corrector would see far richer features, and a cross-objective foundation model
trained on fully matured outcomes~\cite{meta2025gem} is the artefact positioned to supply it. We
expect that to widen the gains reported here, but we did not measure it.
