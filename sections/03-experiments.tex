\paragraph{Data and protocol.} We use the public Criteo conversion logs released
with~\cite{chapelle2014modeling}. Clicks span 60 days. Days $[0,30)$ initialise the model and days
$[30,60)$ form the hourly online training period, divided into \nCohorts{} hourly rounds yielding \nEval{}
scored clicks at a base conversion rate of \baseRate{}. Learning rates are selected per arm and
per cell from a seven-point grid on a held-out slice of the initialisation period, then pinned across seeds. The teacher model's rate is fixed and decoupled from
the serving model's.

\paragraph{Model architecture.} In the model arch backbone, the eight integer fields enter a dense
arm as $\log(1+x)$ paired with an observed-mask bit, normalised and passed through a two-layer
perceptron to a projection vector. The nine categorical fields are hashed per field and
embedded, giving a token per field. Tokens and the dense projection
are then consumed by a stack of Wukong interaction layers~\cite{zhang2024wukong}, then output
the final prediction.

\paragraph{Different setups and evaluation metric.} Two attribution windows ($v\in\{1, 7\}$ days), three
join delays ($o\in\{5,30,90\}$ minutes), four different serving model complexities
($\mathrm{M}_0$ to $\mathrm{M}_3$), and two different teacher models are used. Across the four
complexities the hash width per categorical field grows $36 \to 256 \to 1{,}280 \to 16{,}384$
and the embedding dimension widens $4 \to 4 \to 8 \to 8$, roughly an order of magnitude of
parameters per step, \capMin{} to \capMax{} parameters and \flopMin{} to \flopMax{} MFLOPs per
inference.
For the two teacher models, \emph{Teacher 1} places
8-dimensional embedding tables under a two-layer perceptron, giving \teacherSmallParams{}
parameters at \teacherSmallFlops{} MFLOPs. \emph{Teacher 2} places 48-dimensional tables under a
deep interaction trunk, giving \teacherParams{} parameters at \teacherFlops{} MFLOPs. Running
both is what lets us report the method's sensitivity to the choice of teacher rather than to one
arbitrary configuration of it. For each step, we ran five to ten seeds for statistical analysis.
We report normalized entropy and calibration against mature $\Yv$ for all methods.

\paragraph{With teacher models' prediction as soft target, decoupling the join delay from the attribution window improves accuracy.}
\textsc{Correct-Distill} method, which writes the record early and replaces only its observed negatives with the teacher's estimate, 
beats \textsc{Wait} in \cdBeatWait{} of \nCellsBoth{} cells, by \cdSevenMeanAbs{}\% on average
at $v=7$\,d and
\cdOneMeanAbs{}\% at $v=1$\,d. Figure~\ref{fig:gain} shows where that gain comes from and where
it runs out. Comparing the $v=7$\,d and $v=1$\,d, the gain is largest where the funnel is slowest, because \textsc{Wait} at $v=7$\,d trains on week-old data and no amount of label accuracy compensates for that. Along the model complexity axis, \textsc{Distill-Only}, which relies purely on the teacher models' predcition and carries no
observed label at all, beats \textsc{Wait} in all \doSmallBeat{} of \doSmallN{} cells at the two
smallest capacities, by \doOneSmallAbs{}\% at $v=1$\,d and \doSevenSmallAbs{}\% at $v=7$\,d, and
loses at the two largest by \doOneLargeAbs{}\% and \doSevenLargeAbs{}\%. This trend is plausibly because when a model becomes more complex, it can learn better from the hard targets, then the freshness gain from the soft targets can no longer compensate the information loss from a hard target to a soft target~\cite{menon2021statistical}. However, it can still be applicable for early ranking stages such as retrieval, where the model complexity needs to be low to have a high throughput to rank a large amount of items. In addition, when comparing across different joint delay $o=5, 30$ and $90$ minutes, for $v=7$\,d, larger $o$ improves the model accuracies of \textsc{Correct-Distill}, it is likely to larger $o$ means more hard labels are included.  

Table~\ref{tab:baselines} places both targets against the literature at one operating point,
$v=1$\,d and $o=90$ minutes. Among the methods a single-visit pipeline can actually run we lead
at both rungs, \slSmallOur{}\% against \slSmallUlc{}\% for ULC~\cite{wang2023unbiased} at
$\mathrm{M}_0$ and \slLargeOur{}\% against \slLargeUlc{}\% at $\mathrm{M}_3$, with overlapping
intervals, and we are better calibrated than \textsc{Wait} at both rungs while \textsc{Fresh}
degrades calibration error by \slSmallFreshCalRatio{} and \slLargeFreshCalRatio{} times. The
constraint is not free: \textsc{\slSmallBest} reaches \slSmallBestGain{}\% at $\mathrm{M}_0$ by
writing the same click several times, which supplies training signal that no method with single-visit
constraint can recover.
