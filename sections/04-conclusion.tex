\section{Concluding Remarks}
\label{sec:conclusion}

An objective's attribution window is fixed by the business definition of a conversion, but the
join delay its pipeline adopts is not, and the two can be set independently. Shortening the join
delay by three orders of magnitude improves accuracy rather than degrading it, recovers
\recoverSeven{}\% of what a label-peeking oracle attains at seven days, and helps most where the
funnel is slowest. Which target to write depends on the capacity being served: above the two
smallest rungs the record should retain its observed positive, while at small capacity the
label can be dropped and the teacher's estimate carried alone, which suits retrieval and other
early-funnel stages and is simpler to emit.

The immediate consequence is for training several ranking objectives in one model, an active
industrial direction~\cite{meta2025gem} whose first obstacle is a pipeline obstacle rather than
a modelling one. Under \textsc{Wait}, objective $k$'s record exists only at $C_i+v_k$, so a
shared append-only stream must either write $K$ records per click, violating Rule~2 $K$ times
over, or delay every objective until $\max_k v_k$, imposing the slowest funnel's staleness on
the fastest. A single stream at one short join delay instead carries
$\tilde y_{i,k}=\Yo_{i,k}+(1-\Yo_{i,k})\,\wt_k(x_i)$ for every objective at once, because
$\wt_k$ is a function the pipeline evaluates rather than an outcome it awaits. \textsc{Ulc}
reaches comparable accuracy, so the choice between them is architectural: \textsc{Ulc} fits each
$w_k$ inside the served model's training loop, turning $K$ objectives into $K$ auxiliary heads
on one optimiser, whereas a teacher places each $w_k$ upstream with a single number written onto
a record as its only interface. Section~\ref{sec:exp} shows the result is stable across teacher
architectures and survives freezing the teacher after initialisation, so consolidation can add
$K$ static model files to the build rather than $K$ running training jobs.

Three limits bound the claim. We ran one objective at a time, so we have no evidence on
interference between objectives sharing a trunk, which is the central risk in the multi-task
target above. Our teacher stands in for the production ranking model the argument invokes rather
than being an instance of one. And one corpus with one chronological split measures precision
rather than generalisation. The experiment this makes worth running is $K$ prediction heads,
$K$ attribution windows, one stream, and the teachers placed upstream.
