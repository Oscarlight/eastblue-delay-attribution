Conversion pipelines in advertising are split by speed: an upper-funnel onsite conversion is
observable within minutes of the click, while a down-funnel offsite conversion takes a day or
more. Each objective defines an \emph{attribution window}, the elapsed time within which a
conversion counts, and conventionally that window also fixes the \emph{join delay}, the age at
which the click's training record is written. Tying the two together forces every objective
onto its own pipeline, each as stale as the window it waited for. We ask whether they can be
set independently. Writing records sooner labels every conversion still in flight as a
negative, and a streaming pipeline that trains each click at most once cannot revise them
afterwards, yet most published corrections do exactly that: of \nScreened{} methods we
reproduce, \nFail{} write more than one record per click, up to \maxDup{}. On the Criteo
Delayed Feedback Dataset we compare three targets across \nRunsBoth{} runs, five served
capacities, three join delays, two attribution windows and two teacher architectures. Writing
the record early and softening only its observed negatives with a teacher's estimate beats
waiting for the matured label in \cdBeatWait{} of \nCellsBoth{} configurations, by
\cdSevenMeanAbs{}\% at seven days and \cdOneMeanAbs{}\% at one, which is \recoverSeven{}\% and
\recoverOne{}\% of what a label-peeking oracle attains. It is the strongest method obeying the
one-record rule.
