Conversion pipelines in advertising are split by the delay time a conversion is observable: an upper-funnel onsite conversion is
observable within minutes of the click, while a down-funnel offsite conversion takes a day or
more. Each objective defines an \emph{attribution window}, the elapsed time within which a
conversion counts. Conventionally a training pipeline's join delay is the same as conversion attribution window. Tying the two together forces every objective
onto its own pipeline, each as stale as the attribution window it waited for. We ask whether they can be
set independently. Naively writing training records sooner makes every conversion still in flight become a
negative example, and a streaming pipeline that visits each click at most once cannot revise them
afterwards. Yet most published corrections do exactly that: of \nScreened{} methods we
reproduce, \nFail{} write more than one training record per click, up to \maxDup{}. On the Criteo
Delayed Feedback Dataset we compare three targets across \nRunsBoth{} runs, four served
capacities, three join delays, two attribution windows and two teacher architectures. Writing
the record early and replacing only the negatives with a teacher model's estimate beats
waiting for the matured label in \cdBeatWait{} of \nCellsBoth{} configurations, by
\cdSevenMeanAbs{}\% at seven days and \cdOneMeanAbs{}\% at one, which is \recoverSeven{}\% and
\recoverOne{}\% of what a label-peeking oracle attains. It is the strongest method obeying the
single-visit constraint.
