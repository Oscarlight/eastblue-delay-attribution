Conversion pipelines in advertising are split by how long a conversion takes to observe: an
upper-funnel onsite conversion is visible within minutes of the click, while a down-funnel
offsite conversion takes a day or more. Each objective defines an \emph{attribution window}, the
elapsed time within which a conversion counts, and conventionally a training pipeline's join
delay is set equal to that attribution window. Tying the two together forces every objective
onto its own pipeline, each as stale as the window it waited for. We ask whether they can be set
independently. Writing training records sooner turns every conversion still in flight into a
negative example, and a streaming pipeline that visits each click at most once cannot revise
them afterwards. Yet most published corrections rely on exactly such a revision: of
\nScreened{} methods we reproduce, \nFail{} write more than one training record per click, as
many as \maxDup{}. On the Criteo Delayed Feedback Dataset we compare three targets across
\nRunsBoth{} runs, four served capacities, three join delays, two attribution windows and two
teacher architectures. Writing the record early and replacing only the negatives with a teacher
model's estimate beats waiting for the matured label in \cdBeatWait{} of \nCellsBoth{}
configurations, by \cdSevenMeanAbs{}\% at seven days and \cdOneMeanAbs{}\% at one, which is
\recoverSeven{}\% and \recoverOne{}\% of what a label-peeking oracle attains. It is the
strongest method that obeys the single-visit constraint.
