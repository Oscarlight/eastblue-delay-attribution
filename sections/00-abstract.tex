Conversion pipelines in advertising are split by speed. An upper-funnel onsite conversion is
observable within minutes of the click, while a down-funnel offsite conversion takes a day or
more. Each objective defines an \emph{attribution window}, the elapsed time within which a
conversion counts, and conventionally that window also fixes the \emph{join delay}, the age at
which the click's training record is written. Tying the two together forces every objective onto
its own pipeline, each as stale as the window it waited for. We ask whether they can be set
independently: can an objective accountable to a seven-day window be trained from records joined
within 90 minutes?

Writing records sooner labels every conversion still in flight as a negative, and a streaming
pipeline that trains each click at most once cannot revise them afterwards, yet most published
corrections do exactly that: of \nScreened{} methods we reproduce, \nFail{} write more than one
record per click, up to \maxDup{}. The deficit must instead be filled when the record is
written, by an estimate from a model trained on matured outcomes.

On the Criteo Delayed Feedback Dataset we compare three targets across \nRunsBoth{} runs: the
matured label, denoted \textsc{Wait}; the teacher's estimate alone, \textsc{Distill-Only}; and
the observed label with only its negatives softened, \textsc{Correct-Distill}. We vary served
capacity over \capMin{} to \capMax{} parameters, three join delays, two attribution windows, and
two teacher architectures differing \teacherRatio{}-fold in size. Against a \textsc{Wait}
baseline of $\NE$ \waitNeMin{} to \waitNeMax{}, \textsc{Distill-Only} reduces $\NE$ by
\doOneSmallAbs{}\% at the two smallest capacities but increases it by \doOneLargeAbs{}\% at the
three largest. \textsc{Correct-Distill} improves on \textsc{Distill-Only} in all \nCellsBoth{}
configurations and on \textsc{Wait} in \cdBeatWait{}, reducing $\NE$ by \cdSevenMeanAbs{}\% at
seven days and \cdOneMeanAbs{}\% at one, which is \recoverSeven{}\% and \recoverOne{}\% of what
a label-peeking oracle attains. It is also the strongest method that obeys the one-record rule,
and the larger teacher does not help: it changes the result by \teachCdAbs{}\,pp. Setting the
join delay independently of the attribution window therefore removes the obstacle to training
objectives with different windows on a single fresh stream.
