\section{Implications for Consolidated Pipelines}
\label{sec:consolidation}

Training several ranking objectives in one model is an active industrial
direction~\cite{meta2025gem}, and the obstacle it meets first is a pipeline obstacle rather than
a modelling one. Under \textsc{Wait}, objective $k$'s record exists only at $C_i+v_k$. A shared
append-only stream must therefore either write $K$ records per click, violating Rule~2 $K$ times
over, or delay every objective until $\max_k v_k$, imposing the slowest funnel's staleness on
the fastest.

Our result removes that dilemma. A single stream at one short join delay can carry
\begin{equation}
  \tilde y_{i,k} \;=\; \Yo_{i,k} + (1-\Yo_{i,k})\,\wt_k(x_i)
  \label{eq:multi}
\end{equation}
for every objective at once, because $\wt_k$ is a function the pipeline evaluates rather than an
outcome it awaits. Doing so improves accuracy rather than trading it away, and improves it most
for the slow objectives that forced the separation in the first place.

\textsc{Ulc} is the closest alternative and reaches comparable accuracy, so the choice between
them is architectural. \textsc{Ulc} fits each $w_k$ inside the served model's training loop,
turning $K$ objectives into $K$ auxiliary heads on one optimiser. A teacher instead places each
$w_k$ upstream, with a single number written onto a record as its only interface.
Section~\ref{sec:results} establishes what that upstream model has to be, and the answer is
undemanding on every count that matters for adoption: not large, not computationally expensive,
and not retrained while the student trains. Consolidation therefore adds $K$ static model files
to the build rather than $K$ running training jobs. This is the regime in which an existing
cross-objective ranking model is attractive as the source of $\wt_k$, not because it is large
but because it already exists, already observes matured outcomes for every objective, and can be
queried once per record and then ignored.

\paragraph{What this work does not establish.} We ran one objective at a time, so we have no
evidence on interference between objectives that share a trunk. That is the central risk in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:multi} and the thing a multi-task study must measure. Our teacher also stands in
for the production ranking model the argument invokes rather than being an instance of one.
Finally, one corpus with one chronological split measures precision rather than generalisation.

\paragraph{Conclusion.} An objective's attribution window is fixed by the business definition of
a conversion, but the join delay its pipeline adopts is not. The two can be set independently.
Shortening the join delay by three orders of magnitude improves accuracy rather than degrading
it, recovers \recoverSeven{}\% of what a label-peeking oracle attains at seven days, and helps
most where the funnel is slowest, provided the record retains part of its hard label. The fully
label-free target, which would be the most convenient object for a consolidated pipeline to
carry, is the one that fails. The experiment this makes worth running is $K$ prediction heads,
$K$ attribution windows, one stream, and the teachers placed upstream.
