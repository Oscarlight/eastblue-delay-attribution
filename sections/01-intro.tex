\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Advertising systems predict several kinds of conversion at once, and those kinds differ sharply
in how long they take to observe. A view or an add-to-cart is recorded within minutes of the
click; a purchase, an app install or a submitted lead may take a day or a week. Each objective
is defined with an \emph{attribution window}, the elapsed time within which a conversion is
credited to the click, and a production system maintains several windows at once.
Conventionally that window also determines the \emph{join delay}, the age at which the click's
training record is written to the stream: the pipeline waits until the label is knowable, then
emits the record. The convention costs twice. A model for a slow objective trains only on data
at least a window old, so it never sees recent behaviour. And a system that ranks several
objectives jointly must either maintain one pipeline per objective or delay all of them until
the slowest window closes, imposing the staleness of the slowest funnel on the fastest.

Neither cost is intrinsic. The attribution window is fixed by the business definition of a
conversion; the join delay is a pipeline choice. We ask what happens when the two are set
independently, and specifically whether an objective accountable to a seven-day window can be
trained from records joined within 90 minutes.

The obstacle is that an early record is wrong in a known direction: every conversion still in
flight when the record is written is recorded as a negative. A substantial literature corrects
this censoring
bias~\cite{chapelle2014modeling,yasui2020feedback,yang2021capturing,chen2022asymptotically,ding2026delayed},
but most corrections revise the record afterwards, by duplicating the click when its outcome
lands, by relabelling it, or by adding prediction heads at further horizons. A streaming
pipeline that appends each record once and trains on it once can do none of these. We state
that restriction as a \emph{single-visit} constraint and measure which published methods satisfy
it. What then remains is a choice of what the single frozen target should contain, and the
deficit must be filled at write time by an estimate from a model that has seen matured outcomes.
Such a model is readily available in production, where large ranking models are already trained
across objectives on fully matured data~\cite{meta2025gem}.

The contributions of this work are: (1) a separation of the join delay from the attribution
window, with the causality and single-visit rules a deployable pipeline must satisfy, and an
audit of \nScreened{} published methods against them, of which \nFail{} violate single-visit by
writing up to \maxDup{} records per click; (2) evidence that writing the record early and
softening only its observed negatives beats waiting for the matured label, by more as the funnel
slows, and is the strongest target obeying the single-visit rule; (3) ablations establishing
that the result needs neither a large teacher, an expensive one, nor one retrained while the
student trains, which is what makes it cheap to adopt beside an existing ranking system.
