\section{Experimental specification}\label{app:spec}

\subsection{Admissibility: two hard rules and one practical one}\label{app:rules}

Three rules govern this study. Two are \emph{hard}: violating them means using information that did
not exist, which no amount of engineering can repair. The third is \emph{practical}: it can be
violated, at a cost in pipeline complexity, and drawing the line in the wrong place here was our
own earlier mistake.

\begin{description}
\item[Rule 1a (hard --- label knowability).] A training record emitted after a wait $\Delta$
  carries the label
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:rule1a}
  y_i \;=\; \ind[\,\text{conv\_ts}_i \;\le\; \text{click\_ts}_i + \Delta\,],
  \end{equation}
  whose value must be \emph{knowable at the moment of emission} and is thereafter frozen. A
  conversion arriving later may not flip that record's label, and no update may revise the
  supervision already given for it.

\item[Rule 1b (hard --- teacher soft labels).] A soft target is exempt from~\eqref{eq:rule1a}
  provided the model producing it is itself trained under Rule~1a. The teacher trains only on
  matured records, whose hard labels satisfy~\eqref{eq:rule1a} at $\Delta=v$; it is then
  \emph{evaluated} on the freshest clicks, those released at $\Delta=o$, and the student trains on
  that read-out. The student's record then carries no hard label drawn from its own future, only a
  deterministic function of its features under parameters frozen at emission.

\item[Rule 2 (practical --- one served record per click).] The served model consumes exactly one
  training record per click. This is a property of the serving pipeline, not of information: it
  keeps the dataset append-only, avoids duplicate-key handling, and makes backfills replayable.
\end{description}

\paragraph{Why Rule 2 is practical and not hard.} It is tempting to state Rule~2 as ``a click is
read once'', and we did so in an earlier draft. That is false for every corrected method in this
paper. \textsc{Correct} reads each click \emph{twice}: once from the fresh pipeline at $\Delta=o$,
where it supplies the served record and its realised label, and again from the matured pipeline at
$\Delta=v$, where it supplies a row for fitting $w(x)$. The same is true of \textsc{Reweight}, of
\textsc{Twice}'s delay head, and of both distillation arms. What Rule~2 actually constrains is the
\emph{served model's record stream}; the estimator behind the correction necessarily re-reads the
click once its window has closed, and that second read is what makes these methods work at all.
Stated as an absolute, Rule~2 would eliminate every method except \textsc{Fresh} and \textsc{Wait}
and leave nothing to study.

\paragraph{The causality obligation Rule 1b creates.} Rule~1b is sound only if the teacher is
causal when it scores. At cutoff $T_n$ our teacher has trained on clicks satisfying
$C_i + v \le T_n$ and on nothing else, so every parameter it holds derives from outcomes knowable
at $T_n$; scoring a click released at $T_n$ therefore uses no future information. This is enforced
in code and checked by gate~G1 of \texttt{tr\_verify\_distill.py}, which fails the run if a teacher
update reads a row whose window closes after the current cutoff. What Rule~1b does \emph{not}
permit is training the teacher on the fresh stream: the non-compliant reference that does so
measures $2.08$ served records per click and is reported only as a bound.

\subsection{Vetting every method against Rule 1a}\label{app:compliance}

Rule~1a is the gate that decides what may be run at all, so each method is checked against it
individually. The question in each case is narrow and mechanical: \emph{for every record the
method writes, was that record's label knowable when it was written?} Records per click are
measured over a full 720-hour replay (Table~\ref{tab:spec-compliance}).

\paragraph{\textsc{Fresh} --- satisfies 1a.} One record at $\Delta=o$ with $y=\ind[D_i\le o]$. At
$C_i+o$ the pipeline knows whether the conversion has arrived, so the value is knowable, and it is
never revisited. Measured $1.00$ records per click.

\paragraph{\textsc{Wait} --- satisfies 1a.} One record at $\Delta=v$ with $y=\ind[D_i\le v]$,
emitted only once the window has closed. Knowable by construction. Measured $1.00$.

\paragraph{\textsc{Reweight} (\textsc{Fsiw}) --- satisfies 1a.} The served record is
\textsc{Fresh}'s, untouched. The importance weights come from two classifiers fitted on matured
rows, each of which satisfies~\eqref{eq:rule1a} at $\Delta=v$. No record carries an unknowable
label. Measured $1.00$ served records per click.

\paragraph{\textsc{Twice} --- satisfies 1a.} The CVR tower sees one fresh record with $\Yo$.
Conversion-clock arrivals train a \emph{separate} delay head and create no CVR record; each arrival
is an event observed at the time it is consumed. Measured $1.00$ served records per click.

\paragraph{\textsc{Correct} (\textsc{Ulc}) --- satisfies 1a.} Fresh record with $\Yo$, plus $w(x)$
fitted on matured rows. Both label sets are knowable at their own emission times. Measured $1.00$
served records per click, with a second, non-served read of the same click for the estimator.

\paragraph{\textsc{Correct-Distill} and \textsc{Distill-Only} --- satisfy 1a via 1b.} The teacher
trains only on matured rows and is scored at the fresh release.
\textsc{Distill-Only} is the strongest case: its served record carries no realised label at all, so
Rule~1a is satisfied \emph{vacuously} --- there is nothing for a late conversion to revise
(Proposition~\ref{prop:axis}(ii)). Both measured $1.00$.

\paragraph{\textsc{Oracle} --- \emph{violates} 1a.} The record is emitted at $\Delta=o$ but
labelled $\Yv=\ind[D_i\le v]$ with $v>o$. At $C_i+o$ that value is not knowable: the conversion may
arrive at any point up to $C_i+v$. This is the one arm in the study that reads the future, and it
is retained \emph{only} as a non-causal bound, never as a method. Its record count is $1.00$, which
is precisely why records per click cannot by itself decide admissibility.

\paragraph{\textsc{If-Dfm} --- \emph{violates} 1a.} An influence-function update is applied when a
label reverses, which revises supervision already given for an emitted record. The label is
therefore not frozen, and the revision is driven by information that arrived after emission. The
instrument measures $2.90$ visits per click and up to $99$ for a single click.

\paragraph{The duplicating family --- satisfies 1a, violates Rule 2.} This is the case where our
earlier draft was wrong, and it is worth stating precisely. \textsc{Vanilla}, \textsc{Es-Dfm} and
\textsc{Defuse} ($1.08$ records per click) and \textsc{Ddfm} ($1.89$) write a second record when
the conversion lands. That second record is emitted at $\Delta'=D_i$ and labelled positive, and at
that moment the label \emph{is} knowable --- so, read strictly, each record individually satisfies
Rule~1a. The same holds for the multi-window family, \textsc{Ftp} ($3.00$), \textsc{Miss} ($2.05$)
and \textsc{Pi} ($2.00$), whose heads relabel the same click at their own horizons: each head's
label is knowable at its own horizon. What these methods violate is Rule~2, and they violate it
structurally, because duplication \emph{is} the mechanism rather than an implementation detail.

Two consequences follow, and we state them rather than let the record count imply them. First,
records per click is not a proxy for Rule~1a: \textsc{Oracle} writes one record and violates it,
while \textsc{Ddfm} writes $1.89$ and does not. Second, excluding the duplicating family from the
production run is a \emph{scope} decision --- this paper studies what a single-served-record
pipeline can do --- and not a claim that those methods use unknowable information. Where a system
does support duplication, they remain available, and our audit is then a cost accounting rather
than a prohibition.

\paragraph{What the production run includes.} The seven arms that satisfy both hard rules
(\textsc{Fresh}, \textsc{Reweight}, \textsc{Wait}, \textsc{Twice}, \textsc{Correct},
\textsc{Correct-Distill}, \textsc{Distill-Only}) plus \textsc{Oracle} as an explicitly non-causal
bound. The duplicating and multi-window families are documented above and in
Table~\ref{tab:spec-compliance} but are not run.

\subsection{Common substrate}\label{app:substrate}

\paragraph{Corpus and split.} Criteo-DFD~\cite{chapelle2014modeling}: $15{,}609{,}913$ clicks with
conversion timestamps, of which $3{,}538{,}028$ ever convert at any horizon and the longest
observed delay is $30.1$ days. Pre-train on days $[0,30)$, then stream $[30,60)$ with $719$ hourly
cut-offs. At each cut-off a model consumes the records released in that hour and then predicts
every click of the next hour exactly once, scored retrospectively against $\Yv$.

\paragraph{Seeds do not change the data.} The split, the cut-offs and the per-hour cohorts are
deterministic; a seed varies only parameter initialisation and minibatch order. This was verified
rather than assumed: across all five seeds at a fixed rung the evaluation set is identical
($n_{\text{eval}} = 8{,}196{,}131$, base rate $0.136139$, $719$ cohorts, all bit-identical). There
is therefore one data specification, not five.

\paragraph{Label statistics.} Table~\ref{tab:spec-labels} gives the label composition at both
windows. The arithmetic is asserted in code: positives plus negatives equal $N$, the fresh
positives are a strict subset of the served positives, and the observed rate equals
$\text{base} \times F(o)$ to machine precision.

Two facts drive much of the paper. First, $\Yo$ does not depend on $v$, so the fresh label is
\emph{identical} at both windows ($1{,}649{,}879$ positives); only the target it is asked to
predict moves. Second, censoring deepens with the window --- $F(o)$ falls from $0.769$ at $v=1$\,d
to $0.574$ at $v=7$\,d --- so the fresh label is a good proxy at one day and a poor one at seven.

\subsection{The A0--A4 capacity ladder}\label{app:ladder}

The previous S0--S4 ladder varied only hash rows against a \emph{fixed} trunk of $143{,}293$
parameters, so total capacity spanned just $66\times$ and was trunk-dominated below the top rung;
it was a table-resolution axis described as a capacity axis. A0--A4 pairs a rung of the
compute-efficient frontier with a row count and lets the embedding width rise monotonically,
giving $\sim\!10\times$ per step over $10{,}346\times$. A4 is byte-identical to S4, so the two
ladders share a top rung.

\caption{The A0--A4 ladder, introspected from the instantiated models. The trunk grows with the
tables, and embedding width rises monotonically $2\to4\to8$}
\label{tab:spec-ladder}
\centering\small
\input{appendix/tab_spec_ladder}
\end{table}

\paragraph{Why the width rises.} Two earlier designs were rejected on measurement. Letting each
frontier node keep its own width put A3 at $\text{emb}=48$ with $2048$ rows, which scored
\emph{worse} than A2 at $\text{emb}=8$ with $1280$ rows despite ten times the parameters: on this
corpus resolution carries the signal and width does not. Pinning the width at $8$ everywhere fixed
that but removed the Oracle inversion entirely ($+0.0166 \to -0.0004$ at the bottom rung), because
the inversion needs genuine representational starvation and $\text{emb}=8$ is already wide enough
at $36$ rows. The adopted ladder raises width monotonically, $2\to4\to8$, keeping the starved
bottom without the mid-ladder misallocation.
