\begin{abstract}
Conversion labels arrive days after the click, so a model trained today must either wait for
labels and train on stale data, or use fresh data whose labels are only partly observed. The
standard answer is to correct the censoring. We argue the answer should instead be to distil an
offline teacher, and we give both the theory and the measurements for why. Two ceilings govern the
choice. Censoring correction is bounded by the information remaining in censored labels, a
property of the delay distribution and therefore fixed; we derive that bound in closed form from
the empirical delay distribution alone, before any model is trained, and on the Criteo
delayed-feedback corpus it is $0.868$ of the penalty for a seven-day window. Distillation is
bounded instead by the teacher, which is a training-time asset and can be bought. We test both
with 1{,}600 runs spanning five student capacities, twenty rolling origins and five strategies
under one protocol. Correction attains $0.883$, $0.874$ and $0.849$ at the three smallest
capacities, within $0.02$ of the prediction, confirming the theory and leaving little left to win.
Distillation is not bounded that way: at a one-day window it beats the infeasible oracle at four of
five capacities. Its gain tracks teacher headroom and collapses to $-0.00015$ once that headroom
falls to $0.0036$, making offline teacher scale the one lever here whose supply is not fixed by
the corpus.

\keywords{Delayed feedback \and Conversion rate prediction \and Knowledge distillation \and
Censored labels \and Computational advertising}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Conversion feedback is late. A click can convert minutes or weeks later, so at the moment a
training set is assembled some of its labels are simply not yet determined. A decade of work
treats this as an estimation problem to be solved on the label: wait for labels and accept stale
covariates, or use fresh covariates and correct the resulting censoring, typically through a joint
model of conversion and delay \cite{chapelle2014modeling} or through importance weighting
\cite{ktena2019addressing,yasui2020feedback,yang2021capturing}.

Meanwhile the systems these models live in have changed shape. Industrial advertising stacks
increasingly concentrate model capacity in a single large, centrally trained model, of which
Meta's GEM \cite{meta2025gem} is a recent public example, while the models that actually serve
traffic remain small enough to meet a latency budget. That separation matters here for a specific
reason. Training-time capacity and serving-time capacity have become different budgets, and only
the second is bounded by an SLA. A strategy that converts training-time capacity into serving-time
accuracy is therefore buying with the cheaper currency. Knowledge distillation is exactly such a
strategy, and it has an obvious trajectory: as offline teachers grow towards LLM scale, whatever
distillation buys grows with them.

This paper asks whether that trajectory is worth taking for delayed feedback, and answers it by
separating two ceilings that the literature tends to conflate.

The first is a property of the data. A censoring correction can only use the information that
survives in a partly observed label, and that information is fixed by the delay distribution. We
make this quantitative: under the standard conditional-independence assumption, the recoverable
fraction of the delay penalty is the mean relative Fisher information of the censored rows
(Proposition~\ref{prop:ceiling}), computable from the empirical delay distribution and the
calendar before any model is trained. On the Criteo delayed-feedback corpus it is $0.868$ for a
seven-day window, and measured recovery is $0.883$, $0.874$ and $0.849$ at our three smallest
capacities, within $0.02$ of a formula fitted to nothing. This is a good result for the theory and
a discouraging one for the practice: correction is already at its ceiling, and that ceiling cannot
be raised, because the delay distribution is not ours to change.

The second ceiling is a property of the budget. Distillation is not bounded by the oracle at all
(Proposition~\ref{prop:distil}): every other strategy here fits the student class to some
coarsening of the oracle's own data and therefore cannot beat it, whereas distillation routes its
target through a strictly richer teacher class. That is not a technicality. At $\Delta=1$\,d,
distillation beats the infeasible oracle at four of five capacities, by $+0.0015$ to $+0.0040$
normalised entropy, $p<0.001$. No correction-based strategy exceeds the oracle anywhere at
$\Delta=7$\,d.

We then show the gain is bought with exactly the quantity the LLM-teacher trajectory supplies.
Defining teacher headroom as the teacher's advantage over the waiting baseline, distillation's
gain is roughly flat across a $2.8\times$ range of headroom and then collapses to $-0.00015$ at
the one cell where headroom falls to $+0.0036$. Distillation helps precisely while the teacher is
meaningfully better than the student, and our teacher, at $2{,}262\times$ the student's inference
cost, has already run out of headroom against our largest student. Scaling the teacher is thus not
an optional refinement of this result; it is the only direction in which the result can continue.

This study is a controlled baseline, not a demonstration with an LLM-scale teacher, which we did
not run. Its job is to establish that the mechanism works, to measure what governs it, and to show
that the alternative is already exhausted. Concretely: (i)~a parameter-free ceiling on what
censoring correction can recover, validated to within $0.02$ (Sects.~\ref{sec:theory},
\ref{sec:ceiling-attained}); (ii)~the argument and the evidence that distillation is not subject
to it, including where it exceeds the infeasible oracle (Sect.~\ref{sec:distil-escapes});
(iii)~a measurement of what distillation's advantage is bought with, using a control separating
capacity transfer from delay mitigation that we argue should be mandatory here
(Sects.~\ref{sec:headroom}, \ref{sec:decomp}).

\section{Problem Setting}
\label{sec:setting}

A click occurs at time $t$ with covariates $x$. If it converts, the conversion follows after a
delay $d>0$; write $d=\infty$ for a click that never converts. Under an attribution window
$\Delta$ the prediction target is
\begin{equation}
y \;=\; \ind[d \le \Delta], \qquad \eta(x) \;=\; \Pr(d \le \Delta \mid x).
\label{eq:label}
\end{equation}

The difficulty is not that $y$ is noisy but that it is not yet determined. At an origin $T$, the
moment a training set is assembled, a row clicked at $t$ has been observable for
\begin{equation}
e(t) \;=\; \mathrm{clip}(T-t,\;0,\;\Delta),
\qquad
y_{\mathrm{obs}} \;=\; \ind[d \le e(t)] \;\le\; y .
\label{eq:obs}
\end{equation}
Two regimes follow. If $t \le T-\Delta$ then $e=\Delta$ and $y_{\mathrm{obs}}=y$ exactly. If
$t > T-\Delta$ the row is right-censored: $y_{\mathrm{obs}}=1$ is conclusive, but
$y_{\mathrm{obs}}=0$ is ambiguous, because the click may still convert inside its remaining
window. Everything below follows from \eqref{eq:obs}, and no strategy is granted an assumption
the others lack.

\subsection{Five Strategies as Estimators}

Let $\mathcal{M}=[0,T-\Delta)$ denote the mature rows and $\mathcal{C}=[T-\Delta,T)$ the censored
ones, with $|\mathcal{M}|=m$ and $|\mathcal{C}|=c$. Table~\ref{tab:arms} states each strategy as a
choice of training set and target. Every strategy serves the same student architecture, so they
are indistinguishable in serving cost and differ only in what they train on. The Oracle is
infeasible at time $T$, since it uses labels that do not yet exist; it serves only as the
denominator for how much of the penalty a feasible strategy recovers.

\begin{table}[t]
\caption{The five strategies, with the infeasible Oracle and the distillation control. All are
evaluated on the same held-out day against the same target \eqref{eq:label}, and all serve the
same student}
\label{tab:arms}
\centering
\begin{tabular}{@{}llp{5.0cm}@{}}
\toprule
& training set & target \\
\midrule
Oracle & $\mathcal{M}\cup\mathcal{C}$ & true $y$ (infeasible at $T$) \\
Wait & $\mathcal{M}$ & $y$ \\
Distil & $\mathcal{M}\cup\mathcal{C}$ & teacher's $\hat\eta$; teacher trained on $\mathcal{M}$ \\
Distil-Stale & $\mathcal{M}$ & the same teacher's $\hat\eta$ (control) \\
Correct & $\mathcal{M}\cup\mathcal{C}$ & $y_{\mathrm{obs}}$; loss on $\sigma(z)\hat F(e)$, serve $\sigma(z)$ \\
Band & $[0,T-u_k)$, $k=1\ldots K$ & $\ind[u_{k-1}<d\le u_k]$, predictions summed \\
Reweight & $\mathcal{M}$ & $y$, weighted by $\hat p_{\mathcal{C}}(x)/\hat p_{\mathcal{M}}(x)$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

Correct is the classical joint conversion-delay likelihood of \cite{chapelle2014modeling},
with a non-parametric $\hat F$. Under the conditional-independence assumption
\begin{equation}
\Pr(d \le e \mid x,\ d \le \Delta) \;=\; F(e),
\label{eq:a1}
\end{equation}
that is, delay independent of the covariates given conversion inside the window, the observed
label has mean $\Pr(y_{\mathrm{obs}}=1 \mid x,e)=\eta(x)F(e)$. Fitting $\sigma(z(x))\hat F(e)$
against $y_{\mathrm{obs}}$ under log loss is then a proper composite likelihood for $\eta$. The
correction lives in the loss, and $\sigma(z)$ is served directly with no post-hoc rescaling.

Distil-Stale is not an ablation but a necessary control. Distil changes two things at once
relative to Wait: the student inherits a teacher that may be larger than itself, and it trains on
covariates that Wait had to discard. Distil-Stale removes only the second. Without it, any gain
from Distil is unattributable, and Sect.~\ref{sec:decomp} shows the attribution is not the one a
reader would guess.

\section{Two Ceilings}
\label{sec:theory}

\subsection{What Correction Can Recover}

Under \eqref{eq:a1} a censored observation with window $e$ is Bernoulli with mean $\eta F(e)$. Its
Fisher information about $\eta$ is $I(\eta;e)=(\partial_\eta \eta F)^2/(\eta F(1-\eta F))
= F/(\eta(1-\eta F))$, against $I_{\mathrm{full}}=1/(\eta(1-\eta))$ for a mature row. The relative
information of a censored row is therefore
\begin{equation}
\rho(F) \;=\; \frac{I(\eta;e)}{I_{\mathrm{full}}} \;=\; \frac{F(1-\eta)}{1-\eta F} \;\in\; [0,1],
\qquad \rho(1)=1, \quad \rho(F)\approx F \ \text{for small } \eta .
\label{eq:rho}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:ceiling}
Assume \eqref{eq:a1}, and that excess risk scales as $1/n$ in the effective sample size. Let
$\bar\rho = |\mathcal{C}|^{-1}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{C}}\rho(F(e_i))$. Then the fraction of the
Oracle-minus-Wait gap recovered by Correct is
\begin{equation}
R \;=\; \frac{1/m - 1/(m+\bar\rho c)}{1/m - 1/(m+c)} \;=\; \frac{\bar\rho\,(m+c)}{m+\bar\rho c}.
\label{eq:recovery}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

$R$ contains no fitted quantity: $\bar\rho$ follows from the empirical delay distribution and the
base rate, and $m$ and $c$ from the calendar. It can be computed before training anything.

On this corpus the delay distribution is strongly front-loaded, with $57.4\,\%$ of conversions
within seven days landing in the first hour, so censored rows retain most of their information
even when their window has barely opened. Averaged over the twenty origins used below,
$\bar\rho=0.886$ and $R=0.888$ for $\Delta=1$\,d, and $\bar\rho=0.847$ and $R=0.868$ for
$\Delta=7$\,d.

\subsection{Why Distillation Is Not Subject to It}

Proposition~\ref{prop:ceiling} prices one strategy, but the Oracle bounds a whole family.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:distil}
Let $\mathcal{F}$ be the student class. Consider any strategy that fits $\mathcal{F}$ to a target
measurable with respect to the Oracle's own training data. Asymptotically its risk is at least the
Oracle's, so its recovery satisfies $R \le 1$. Correct, Band and Reweight are of this form.
Distil is not: its target is produced by a fit in a strictly richer class
$\mathcal{G}\supset\mathcal{F}$. As the teacher approaches $\eta$, Distil's limiting risk is the
approximation error of $\mathcal{F}$ alone, whereas the Oracle additionally carries an estimation
term of order $1/(m+c)$. Hence $R>1$ is attainable for Distil and not for the others.
\end{proposition}

The first half is a data-processing argument: $y_{\mathrm{obs}}$, the band indicators and
Reweight's weights are all functions of $(x,y,d)$ observed on a subset of the Oracle's rows, so
each is a coarsening of the Oracle's signal and no fit of $\mathcal{F}$ to it can do better
asymptotically. The second half is the familiar approximation-estimation split. Distillation does
not add information; the teacher is trained on $\mathcal{M}$, which the Oracle also sees. It
substitutes a better \emph{estimator} of $\eta$, computed in a class the student could not afford
to serve, and hands the student a denoised regression target in place of binary labels.

This is the asymmetry that motivates offline teachers. Proposition~\ref{prop:ceiling}'s ceiling is
set by $F$, a property of the advertisers and the corpus. Proposition~\ref{prop:distil}'s ceiling
is set by $\mathcal{G}$, a property of the training budget. Only the second is something a
practitioner can purchase, and the industrial trend towards large centrally trained models
\cite{meta2025gem} is precisely a supply of it.

Both propositions are asymptotic and ignore optimisation error; Prop.~\ref{prop:ceiling}
additionally assumes \eqref{eq:a1} and attributes the whole penalty to effective sample size.
Section~\ref{sec:results} shows where each idealisation is visible in the data.

\section{Experimental Design}
\label{sec:design}

\paragraph{Protocol.}
We use rolling origins $T=40,\ldots,59$, each with training windows ending at $T$ minus that
strategy's own wait, and evaluation on the single day $[(T{+}1)\mathrm{d},(T{+}2)\mathrm{d})$ with
one idle day in between, held fixed as the window slides. The label \eqref{eq:label} is enforced
identically in training and evaluation: a row may enter a training set only if its label is
knowable at $T$. Evaluation days carry 230k to 300k rows. Conversions are logged to day $90.955$
while clicks stop at day $61.001$, so every evaluation label is fully resolved at every $\Delta$.
The origin is the unit of replication and all comparisons are paired on it, because the evaluation
positive rate swings from $0.1595$ to $0.1966$ across these twenty days, an order of magnitude
more than any effect we measure.

\paragraph{Tasks.}
We use $\Delta\in\{1\mathrm{d},7\mathrm{d}\}$ and drop $\Delta=1$\,h, whose Oracle gap is
$-0.00002$ ($p=0.842$): there is no penalty to recover, so every strategy ties with Wait by
construction.

\paragraph{Capacity Ladder.}
Strategy rankings that hold at one model size need not hold at another, so the entire comparison
is repeated at five capacities $S_0,\ldots,S_4$. All five share one backbone and differ only in
the number of categorical hash-table rows ($36$, $64$, $160$, $896$ and $131{,}072$, or $2{,}592$
to $9{,}437{,}184$ table parameters, a $3{,}641\times$ range), a single monotone knob that is
exactly the embedding-table memory footprint and therefore the binding constraint in production
ranking. The ladder was constructed to be approximately equally spaced in normalised entropy
(mean step $0.0149$, from $0.78626$ at $S_0$ to $0.72794$ at $S_4$ at one reference origin) and is
monotone in it at 40 of 40 (task, origin) cells.

\paragraph{Teacher.}
Distil always uses the same teacher, selected as the best of a 21-rung architecture ladder and
held at full table resolution: $57.3$\,M parameters and $1.59$\,M FLOPs per example, against $704$
FLOPs for every student, a ratio of $2{,}262\times$. It is a training-only asset in exactly the
sense Sect.~\ref{sec:intro} describes, and it could not be served under the student's budget. The
teacher sees Wait's window and Wait's labels, so it differs from Wait on capacity alone, receiving
no extra data and no extra label information. We define its \emph{headroom} as
$H=\NE(\text{Wait})-\NE(\text{teacher})$, the quantity Sect.~\ref{sec:headroom} shows governs
Distil.

\paragraph{Metrics.}
We report normalised entropy $\NE=\text{logloss}/H(\bar y)$, the calibration ratio $\bar p/\bar y$,
and a calibration-adjusted $\NE$ obtained by multiplying every prediction by $c=\bar y/\bar p$, so
that calibration is exactly one, and recomputing $\NE$. That quantity can exceed $\NE$, because
matching the mean is not the same as minimising log loss; it is a level-matching diagnostic, not a
lower bound. All models train for exactly one epoch: a second epoch already overfits at every
capacity (train $-0.0186$, evaluation $+0.0019$, $p=0.025$).

\paragraph{Scale and Verification.}
The grid is 2 tasks $\times$ 5 capacities $\times$ 20 origins $\times$ 8 arms, or 1{,}600 scored
runs. Four gates were checked before launch: the calibration multiplier forces calibration to one
at every starting bias; the harness reproduces previously logged cells bit-exactly (14 of 14); the
shared teacher is identical across capacities to seven decimal places while students are not; and
every arm at a given (task, origin) is scored on an identical evaluation set.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{The Penalty Depends on the Window, Not Much on Capacity}

The Oracle-minus-Wait gap at $\Delta=7$\,d is $+0.00538$, $+0.00602$, $+0.00746$, $+0.00842$
and $+0.00598$ for $S_0$ to $S_4$, all with $p<0.001$ over 20 of 20 origins. At $\Delta=1$\,d it
is an order of magnitude smaller, from $+0.00067$ to $+0.00092$, though equally consistent
(20 of 20, $p<0.001$). Waiting is expensive only when the window is long relative to the
available history: at seven days it withholds $12.6$ to $18.6\,\%$ of the corpus. Because the
$\Delta=1$\,d denominator is below $0.001$ $\NE$, we report absolute differences at that window
and treat recovery \emph{ratios} there as indicative only.

\subsection{Correction Attains Its Ceiling, and Then Falls Below It}
\label{sec:ceiling-attained}

\begin{figure}[t]
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figs/fig1_recovery.pdf}
\caption{The two ceilings. Correct (open circles) tracks the parameter-free prediction of
Proposition~\ref{prop:ceiling} (dashed) at the three smallest capacities and never crosses the
Oracle line at $1.0$. Distil (grey squares) is not governed by either bound: it exceeds the Oracle
throughout $\Delta{=}1$d, and collapses at $S_4$ for $\Delta{=}7$d, where the teacher has almost
no headroom left}
\label{fig:recovery}
\end{figure}

For $\Delta=7$\,d the predicted ceiling is $0.868$, and Correct measures $0.883$, $0.874$,
$0.849$, $0.759$ and $0.625$ across $S_0$ through $S_4$. At the three smallest capacities the
prediction is accurate to $+0.015$, $+0.006$ and $-0.019$, from a formula with no free parameters
that was fitted to nothing. At $S_3$ and $S_4$ it over-predicts by $0.11$ and $0.24$.

The shortfall is misspecification, not estimation. Proposition~\ref{prop:ceiling} assumes
\eqref{eq:a1}, and that assumption is false on this corpus in a way we can measure. Stratifying
converted rows by the distinct values of a single categorical field gives six strata holding
$23{,}790$ to $860{,}851$ conversions each, across which the share of within-seven-day conversions
landing in the first hour ranges from $0.453$ to $0.689$ (pooled: $0.574$) and the median delay
from $0.16$\,h to $1.73$\,h, a tenfold spread. A model too small to resolve
covariate-dependent delay cannot be harmed by a correction that assumes it away, so at $S_0$
through $S_2$ the only cost of censoring is the statistical one that
Proposition~\ref{prop:ceiling} prices exactly. As capacity grows, the misspecification bias in
$\hat F(e)$ becomes expressible and recovery falls below the ceiling. The prediction is therefore
best read as an upper bound that is tight precisely when the model is sample-limited rather than
bias-limited, and the gap between prediction and measurement estimates what a
covariate-conditional delay model would buy.

The practical reading is the same either way: where the ceiling is attained nothing is left for a
better correction to win, and where it is not, the remedy is a covariate-conditional delay model
rather than a tighter estimator. Both roads end at a bound set by $F$.

\subsection{Distillation Is Not Bounded by the Oracle}
\label{sec:distil-escapes}

No correction-based strategy exceeds the Oracle at $\Delta=7$\,d: Correct is below it at every
capacity by $0.00063$ to $0.00224$ ($p<0.005$), Band by $0.0035$ to $0.0082$, and Reweight by
$0.0039$ to $0.0081$, all $p<0.001$. This is Proposition~\ref{prop:distil}'s first half,
and Correct's placement just under the bound is consistent with it being an efficient rather than
a wasteful estimator.

Distil behaves differently in both windows. At $\Delta=1$\,d it beats the infeasible Oracle at
$S_0$ through $S_3$ by $+0.00150$, $+0.00294$, $+0.00269$ and $+0.00397$, all $p<0.001$, and by
$+0.00071$ ($p=0.06$) at $S_4$. At $\Delta=7$\,d it is statistically indistinguishable from the
Oracle at $S_0$ through $S_3$ ($p=0.45$ to $0.73$) while every other strategy is significantly
below it. Against Wait it gains $+0.00217$ to $+0.00484$ at $\Delta=1$\,d and $+0.00419$ to
$+0.00722$ at $\Delta=7$\,d over $S_0$ through $S_3$, all $p<0.001$ at 19 or 20 of 20 origins.

Head to head with Correct, Distil wins outright at the short window and ties at the long one: it
is ahead by $+0.00150$ to $+0.00411$ at $S_0$ through $S_3$ for $\Delta=1$\,d ($p<0.001$, 20 of 20
origins) and by $+0.00089$ at $S_4$ ($p=0.019$), but the two are within $\pm0.0020$ with no
significant difference at $\Delta=7$\,d ($p=0.25$ to $0.80$). The asymmetry follows from
Sect.~\ref{sec:ceiling-attained}: at seven days Correct is near a ceiling worth $0.868$ of a large
penalty, whereas at one day the penalty is small and the teacher's advantage is not.

One result cuts against Proposition~\ref{prop:distil} and we report it rather than reconcile it.
At $\Delta=1$\,d, Reweight also exceeds the Oracle at $S_0$ ($+0.00199$) and $S_2$ ($+0.00041$),
$p<0.001$. The proposition is asymptotic, and Reweight trains on strictly less data under
non-uniform weights, which regularises; at a window whose entire penalty is under $0.001$ $\NE$ we
read these as finite-sample effects. They do not recur at $\Delta=7$\,d, where Reweight is below
the Oracle at all five capacities.

\subsection{The Gain Is Bought With Teacher Headroom}
\label{sec:headroom}

\begin{figure}[t]
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figs/fig2_penalty_decomp.pdf}
\caption{(a)~Distil's gain against teacher headroom $H$ (log axis), one point per capacity and
window. Gain is roughly flat while $H$ falls by $2.8\times$, then collapses at the single cell
where $H$ reaches $+0.0036$. (b)~Distil decomposed with the Distil-Stale control at
$\Delta{=}7$d. The fresher-covariate term is $47$ to $78\,\%$ of the gain}
\label{fig:decomp}
\end{figure}

Teacher headroom $H$ falls monotonically as the student grows, from $+0.0572$ at $S_0$ to
$+0.0036$ at $S_4$ for $\Delta=7$\,d, because the teacher is fixed while the student closes on it.
Distil's gain does not follow $H$ down. Across $S_1$ to $S_3$, where $H$ falls by $2.8\times$ from
$0.0484$ to $0.0172$, the gain is essentially flat at $+0.00722$, $+0.00682$ and $+0.00709$: a
larger student extracts a larger fraction of a smaller advantage, with transfer efficiency
$\text{gain}/H$ rising from $14.9\,\%$ to $41.2\,\%$ (and from $7.6\,\%$ to $31.0\,\%$ at
$\Delta=1$\,d).

The flatness ends abruptly. At $S_4$, $\Delta=7$\,d, headroom is $+0.0036$ and Distil collapses to
$-0.00015$ ($p=0.898$) --- the only non-positive cell in the grid, and the only one where the
Distil-Stale control is itself negative ($-0.00126$), meaning the teacher is actively degrading
the target. Distillation helps exactly while the teacher is meaningfully better than the student.
That is a statement about model size, not about feedback delay, and it is the boundary condition
that makes teacher scale the operative variable: our teacher, at $2{,}262\times$ the student's
inference cost, is already spent against the largest student we tested.

\subsection{What Distillation Is Actually Buying}
\label{sec:decomp}

Read alone, Distil's win over Wait looks like delay mitigation. The Distil-Stale control shows it
is only partly that. Capacity transfer accounts for $22$ to $53\,\%$ of the gain at $\Delta=7$\,d
across $S_0$ through $S_3$, and $71$ to $94\,\%$ at $\Delta=1$\,d (Fig.~\ref{fig:decomp}b). The
residual, the student's exposure to covariates that Wait discarded and which carry no new label
information, is what is attributable to the delayed label; it is significant at every capacity
($p\le0.0013$, 19 or 20 of 20 origins) and worth $+0.0033$ to $+0.0045$ at $\Delta=7$\,d.

We stress this because the decomposition is not visible without the control, and its two halves
point in the same practical direction. The delay-attributable half is real and is the largest such
term in our grid. The capacity half is the part that grows with the teacher. Reporting Distil
against Wait alone would overstate delay mitigation by a factor of two to four at $\Delta=1$\,d;
we would encourage this literature to treat a Distil-Stale-style control as mandatory.

\subsection{Band and Reweight Do Not Repay Their Complexity}

Band ranges from $-0.00215$ to $+0.00457$ with no monotone pattern in capacity and is significant
in only 5 of 10 cells; its slowest band still trains on Wait's window, so it never escapes the wait
it was designed to avoid. Reweight stays within $-0.00055$ to $+0.00265$ of Wait everywhere,
consistent with the penalty being missing labels rather than covariate staleness --- and Reweight
by construction never touches an unresolved label.

\section{Limitations and Outlook}

The central extrapolation of this paper is untested. We show that Distil's gain is governed by
teacher headroom and that our teacher's headroom is exhausted at $S_4$; we do not show what an
LLM-scale teacher would deliver, because we did not run one. What the grid establishes is that the
mechanism is real, that it is not bounded by the quantity that bounds every alternative here, and
that the alternative is already at its own ceiling. Whether the trend continues at three more
orders of magnitude of teacher is the experiment this baseline is meant to justify, not one it
performs.

On the measurements: we use one seed per origin, with the origin as the unit of replication
($n=20$); seed sensitivity was characterised separately at a standard deviation of $0.0004$ to
$0.0030$, an order of magnitude below the $\Delta=7$\,d effects, but seeds are not averaged here,
and the twenty origins share training data heavily, so the effective sample size is below twenty.
Proposition~\ref{prop:ceiling} assumes a $1/n$ excess-risk rate that we do not verify directly,
its empirical accuracy at $S_0$ through $S_2$ being the evidence offered;
Proposition~\ref{prop:distil} is asymptotic, and Sect.~\ref{sec:distil-escapes} reports a
finite-sample exception to it. The calibration multiplier is fitted on evaluation labels and is a
diagnostic, never an achievable score. Distil's soft targets are largely teacher in-sample on
$\mathcal{M}$, and out-of-fold targets, the standard remedy, are untested. Finally, all results
come from a single public corpus with one delay distribution, so the ceiling of
Proposition~\ref{prop:ceiling} is corpus-specific even though the formula is not.
