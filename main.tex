\begin{abstract}
Ads retrieval must score on the order of $10^6$ candidates within milliseconds, which forces a
single small model to serve every advertiser objective at once. Those objectives carry different
conversion attribution windows, and a hard attribution label is knowable only after its window
closes. Consolidation therefore pins every fast objective to the slowest objective's clock. We
observe that a teacher's soft label has no attribution window, so supervising the consolidated
model with a foundation model decouples the training window from the attribution window entirely.
We test the delayed-label half of this claim on a public corpus with 1{,}600 runs spanning five
serving-scale capacities, twenty rolling origins and five strategies. Two ceilings separate the
options. Censoring correction is capped by the Fisher information surviving in a censored label,
which we derive in closed form from the empirical delay distribution before training anything; it
predicts $0.868$ for a seven-day window and measurement gives $0.883$, $0.874$ and $0.849$.
Distillation is not subject to that cap, beating the infeasible oracle at four of five capacities
for a one-day window, and its gain is governed by teacher headroom, vanishing when headroom does.
Calibration, which ads auctions price on directly, transfers from teacher to student faithfully
and so does its instability: the student inherits the teacher's per-day calibration error rather
than estimating its own.

\keywords{Delayed feedback \and Conversion rate prediction \and Knowledge distillation \and
Foundation models \and Computational advertising}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

An ads retrieval stage has to reduce an inventory of order $10^6$ candidates to a shortlist within
a latency budget of order $10$\,ms. That budget is what makes the stage hard, and it has three
consequences that together frame this paper.

\emph{First, the ranking quantity is a price, not an order.} Candidates are ranked by expected
value, the product of a paced bid and a predicted conversion rate. An error in the predicted rate
is an error in what the advertiser pays, so the estimate must be calibrated and not merely
correctly ordered. Metrics that are invariant to monotone rescaling, such as AUC, do not certify
this.

\emph{Second, advertisers do not share one objective.} A production system optimises many
down-funnel conversion events, and each carries its own attribution window $\Delta$: an install
counted within a day, a purchase within a week. The conventional response is one model per
objective. At retrieval QPS that response does not survive the latency budget, so the objectives
must be consolidated into a single model with one shared trunk and per-objective heads.

\emph{Third, consolidation is obstructed by the label, not by the architecture.} Two issues recur.
The attribution label $\ind[d\le\Delta]$ is not knowable until $\Delta$ has elapsed after the
click, so a shared training window must end at $T-\max_k\Delta_k$: consolidating $K$ objectives
pins every fast objective to the slowest one's clock. And down-funnel events are orders of
magnitude sparser than upper-funnel ones, so a shared trunk trained on hard labels is dominated by
whichever objective is densest. Both are properties of the supervision signal.

This suggests the paradigm shift we study: supervise the consolidated model not with hard
attribution labels but with the soft predictions of a large foundation model trained offline. A
soft label $\hat\eta(x)$ is available at click time, carries no attribution window, and is dense on
every row, so in principle it dissolves both obstructions at once. The pattern is deployed. Meta's
GEM is an LLM-scale ads foundation model trained across thousands of GPUs whose knowledge is
transferred by distillation, representation learning and parameter sharing into hundreds of
latency-sensitive vertical models, lifting conversions by $5\,\%$ on Instagram and $3\,\%$ on
Facebook Feed \cite{meta2025gem}; that report also names the failure mode, \emph{stale
supervision} when the teacher's signal lags ground truth, and adds a component realigning teacher
outputs to recent data.

This paper isolates the delayed-label half of the claim. We fix one objective and vary its
attribution window, the axis consolidation manipulates, across five serving-scale capacities. We do
not test multi-objective consolidation or sparsity imbalance; those motivate the design and bound
what we conclude (Sect.~\ref{sec:limits}).

Our findings separate two ceilings that the delayed-feedback literature conflates. The first is a
property of the data and the second of the budget, and only the second can be bought.
Specifically: (i)~censoring correction is capped by the Fisher information surviving in a censored
label, which we derive in closed form (Proposition~\ref{prop:ceiling}) and validate to within
$0.02$ --- so correction is already at a ceiling that the delay distribution, not the modeller,
fixes; (ii)~distillation is subject to no such cap (Proposition~\ref{prop:distil}), beating the
infeasible oracle at four of five capacities for a one-day window; (iii)~its gain is bought with
teacher headroom and vanishes when headroom does, and our teacher is already spent against our
largest student, which is the concrete sense in which teacher scale is the operative variable; and
(iv)~calibration transfers from teacher to student faithfully, instability included, which makes
per-period teacher calibration a requirement rather than a refinement wherever predictions are
prices.

\section{Problem Setting}
\label{sec:setting}

A click occurs at time $t$ with covariates $x$. If it converts, the conversion follows after a
delay $d>0$; write $d=\infty$ for a click that never converts. Under attribution window $\Delta$
the target is
\begin{equation}
y \;=\; \ind[d \le \Delta], \qquad \eta(x) \;=\; \Pr(d \le \Delta \mid x),
\label{eq:label}
\end{equation}
and the served quantity is a price proportional to $\eta(x)$, so the level of $\hat\eta$ matters
and not only its ordering.

The difficulty is not that $y$ is noisy but that it is not yet determined. At an origin $T$, the
moment a training set is assembled, a row clicked at $t$ has been observable for
\begin{equation}
e(t) \;=\; \mathrm{clip}(T-t,\;0,\;\Delta),
\qquad
y_{\mathrm{obs}} \;=\; \ind[d \le e(t)] \;\le\; y .
\label{eq:obs}
\end{equation}
If $t \le T-\Delta$ then $e=\Delta$ and $y_{\mathrm{obs}}=y$ exactly. If $t > T-\Delta$ the row is
right-censored: $y_{\mathrm{obs}}=1$ is conclusive, but $y_{\mathrm{obs}}=0$ is ambiguous, because
the click may still convert inside its remaining window.

\subsection{Why Consolidation Couples the Windows}

Equation~\eqref{eq:obs} has an immediate consequence for the multi-objective setting that motivates
this work. A model head for objective $k$ can be trained on hard labels only where those labels are
determined, that is on $[0,T-\Delta_k)$. A consolidated model sharing one trunk and one training
stream must therefore draw from
\begin{equation}
W_{\mathrm{hard}} \;=\; \bigcap_{k=1}^{K}\,[0,\;T-\Delta_k) \;=\; [0,\;T-\textstyle\max_k \Delta_k),
\qquad
W_{\mathrm{soft}} \;=\; [0,\;T),
\label{eq:coupling}
\end{equation}
where $W_{\mathrm{soft}}$ is the window available when the supervision is a teacher's prediction
$\hat\eta_k(x)$, which is defined at click time for every $k$. The statement is elementary, but it
is binding: under hard labels the fastest objective inherits the slowest objective's staleness,
and the cost is paid by every head. Under soft labels the intersection disappears. This is the
sense in which distillation is not one delayed-feedback remedy among several but the one that
removes the coupling rather than compensating for it.

Our experiments fix $K=1$ and vary $\Delta$, which isolates the per-objective cost of sitting on a
longer clock without confounding it with multi-task optimisation. The two windows we use bracket
the relevant range: waiting withholds $2.2\,\%$ of the corpus at $\Delta=1$\,d and $15.5\,\%$ at
$\Delta=7$\,d.

\subsection{Five Strategies as Estimators}

Let $\mathcal{M}=[0,T-\Delta)$ denote the mature rows and $\mathcal{C}=[T-\Delta,T)$ the censored
ones, with $|\mathcal{M}|=m$ and $|\mathcal{C}|=c$. Table~\ref{tab:arms} states each strategy as a
choice of training set and target. Every strategy serves the same student, so they are
indistinguishable in serving cost and differ only in supervision. The Oracle is infeasible at time
$T$, since it uses labels that do not yet exist; it is the denominator for how much of the penalty
a feasible strategy recovers.

\begin{table}[t]
\caption{The five strategies, with the infeasible Oracle and the distillation control. All are
evaluated on the same held-out day against the same target \eqref{eq:label}, and all serve the
same student}
\label{tab:arms}
\centering
\begin{tabular}{@{}llp{5.0cm}@{}}
\toprule
& training set & target \\
\midrule
Oracle & $\mathcal{M}\cup\mathcal{C}$ & true $y$ (infeasible at $T$) \\
Wait & $\mathcal{M}$ & $y$ \\
Distil & $\mathcal{M}\cup\mathcal{C}$ & teacher's $\hat\eta$; teacher trained on $\mathcal{M}$ \\
Distil-Stale & $\mathcal{M}$ & the same teacher's $\hat\eta$ (control) \\
Correct & $\mathcal{M}\cup\mathcal{C}$ & $y_{\mathrm{obs}}$; loss on $\sigma(z)\hat F(e)$, serve $\sigma(z)$ \\
Band & $[0,T-u_k)$, $k=1\ldots K$ & $\ind[u_{k-1}<d\le u_k]$, predictions summed \\

\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

Correct is the classical joint conversion-delay likelihood of \cite{chapelle2014modeling}, with a
non-parametric $\hat F$. Under the conditional-independence assumption
\begin{equation}
\Pr(d \le e \mid x,\ d \le \Delta) \;=\; F(e),
\label{eq:a1}
\end{equation}
the observed label has mean $\Pr(y_{\mathrm{obs}}=1 \mid x,e)=\eta(x)F(e)$, so fitting
$\sigma(z(x))\hat F(e)$ against $y_{\mathrm{obs}}$ under log loss is a proper composite likelihood
for $\eta$. The correction lives in the loss, and $\sigma(z)$ is served with no post-hoc rescaling.

Distil-Stale is not an ablation but a necessary control. Distil changes two things at once relative
to Wait: the student inherits a teacher larger than itself, and it trains on covariates Wait had to
discard. Distil-Stale removes only the second. It is the experimental counterpart of the stale
supervision reported in deployment \cite{meta2025gem}, and Sect.~\ref{sec:decomp} shows the
attribution is not the one a reader would guess.

\section{Two Ceilings}
\label{sec:theory}

\subsection{What Correction Can Recover}

Under \eqref{eq:a1} a censored observation with window $e$ is Bernoulli with mean $\eta F(e)$. Its
Fisher information about $\eta$ is $I(\eta;e)=(\partial_\eta \eta F)^2/(\eta F(1-\eta F))
= F/(\eta(1-\eta F))$, against $I_{\mathrm{full}}=1/(\eta(1-\eta))$ for a mature row, so the
relative information of a censored row is
\begin{equation}
\rho(F) \;=\; \frac{F(1-\eta)}{1-\eta F} \;\in\; [0,1],
\qquad \rho(1)=1, \quad \rho(F)\approx F \ \text{for small } \eta .
\label{eq:rho}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:ceiling}
Assume \eqref{eq:a1}, and that excess risk scales as $1/n$ in the effective sample size. Let
$\bar\rho = |\mathcal{C}|^{-1}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{C}}\rho(F(e_i))$. Then the fraction of the
Oracle-minus-Wait gap recovered by Correct is
\begin{equation}
R \;=\; \frac{1/m - 1/(m+\bar\rho c)}{1/m - 1/(m+c)} \;=\; \frac{\bar\rho\,(m+c)}{m+\bar\rho c}.
\label{eq:recovery}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

$R$ contains no fitted quantity: $\bar\rho$ follows from the empirical delay distribution and the
base rate, and $m$ and $c$ from the calendar. On this corpus the delay distribution is strongly
front-loaded, with $57.4\,\%$ of conversions within seven days landing in the first hour, so
censored rows retain most of their information even when their window has barely opened. Over the
twenty origins used below, $\bar\rho=0.886$ and $R=0.888$ for $\Delta=1$\,d, and $\bar\rho=0.847$
and $R=0.868$ for $\Delta=7$\,d.

\subsection{Why Distillation Is Not Subject to It}

Proposition~\ref{prop:ceiling} prices one strategy, but the Oracle bounds a whole family.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:distil}
Let $\mathcal{F}$ be the student class. Consider any strategy that fits $\mathcal{F}$ to a target
measurable with respect to the Oracle's own training data. Asymptotically its risk is at least the
Oracle's, so its recovery satisfies $R \le 1$. Correct, Band and Reweight are of this form.
Distil is not: its target is produced by a fit in a strictly richer class
$\mathcal{G}\supset\mathcal{F}$. As the teacher approaches $\eta$, Distil's limiting risk is the
approximation error of $\mathcal{F}$ alone, whereas the Oracle additionally carries an estimation
term of order $1/(m+c)$. Hence $R>1$ is attainable for Distil and not for the others.
\end{proposition}

The first half is a data-processing argument: $y_{\mathrm{obs}}$, the band indicators and
Reweight's weights are functions of $(x,y,d)$ observed on a subset of the Oracle's rows, so each is
a coarsening of the Oracle's signal and no fit of $\mathcal{F}$ to it can do better asymptotically.
The second half is the approximation-estimation split. Distillation adds no information; the
teacher trains on $\mathcal{M}$, which the Oracle also sees. It substitutes a better
\emph{estimator} of $\eta$, computed in a class the student could not afford to serve, and hands
the student a denoised regression target in place of binary labels.

This is the asymmetry that motivates foundation-model teachers, and it composes with
\eqref{eq:coupling}. The ceiling in Proposition~\ref{prop:ceiling} is set by $F$, a property of
advertisers and users. The ceiling in Proposition~\ref{prop:distil} is set by $\mathcal{G}$, a
property of the offline training budget. Only the second can be purchased, and industrial ads
foundation models are a supply of it \cite{meta2025gem}. Both propositions are asymptotic and
ignore optimisation error; Prop.~\ref{prop:ceiling} additionally assumes \eqref{eq:a1}.

\section{Experimental Design}
\label{sec:design}

\paragraph{Protocol.}
We use rolling origins $T=40,\ldots,59$, each with training windows ending at $T$ minus that
strategy's own wait, and evaluation on the single day $[(T{+}1)\mathrm{d},(T{+}2)\mathrm{d})$ with
one idle day in between, held fixed as the window slides. Label \eqref{eq:label} is enforced
identically in training and evaluation: a row may enter a training set only if its label is
knowable at $T$. Evaluation days carry 230k to 300k rows, and every evaluation label is fully
resolved at every $\Delta$ (conversions are logged to day $90.955$, clicks stop at $61.001$). The
origin is the unit of replication and all comparisons are paired on it, because the evaluation
positive rate swings from $0.1595$ to $0.1966$ across these twenty days --- an order of magnitude
more than any effect we measure.

\paragraph{Tasks.}
We use $\Delta\in\{1\mathrm{d},7\mathrm{d}\}$ and drop $\Delta=1$\,h, whose Oracle gap is
$-0.00002$ ($p=0.842$): there is no penalty to recover, so every strategy ties with Wait by
construction.

\paragraph{Capacity Ladder.}
The premise is that the student stays inside a serving budget, so the entire comparison is repeated
at five capacities $S_0,\ldots,S_4$. All five share one backbone and differ only in the number of
categorical hash-table rows ($36$, $64$, $160$, $896$ and $131{,}072$, or $2{,}592$ to
$9{,}437{,}184$ table parameters, a $3{,}641\times$ range), a single monotone knob that is exactly
the embedding-table memory footprint and therefore the binding constraint in production retrieval.
The ladder was constructed to be approximately equally spaced in normalised entropy (mean step
$0.0149$, from $0.78626$ at $S_0$ to $0.72794$ at $S_4$ at one reference origin) and is monotone in
it at 40 of 40 (task, origin) cells.

\paragraph{Teacher.}
Distil always uses the same teacher, selected as the best of a 21-rung architecture ladder and held
at full table resolution: $57.3$\,M parameters and $1.59$\,M FLOPs per example, against $704$ FLOPs
for every student, a ratio of $2{,}262\times$. It is a training-only asset and could not be served
under the student's budget. It sees Wait's window and Wait's labels, so it differs from Wait on
capacity alone. We define its \emph{headroom} as $H=\NE(\text{Wait})-\NE(\text{teacher})$.

\paragraph{Metrics.}
We report normalised entropy $\NE=\text{logloss}/H(\bar y)$ and the calibration ratio
$\bar p/\bar y$. Because an auction prices on the day it runs, we summarise calibration
\emph{per origin} and then average, rather than averaging the ratio first: the two differ sharply
(Sect.~\ref{sec:calib}). All models train for exactly one epoch; a second epoch already overfits at
every capacity (train $-0.0186$, evaluation $+0.0019$, $p=0.025$).

\paragraph{Scale and Verification.}
The grid is 2 tasks $\times$ 5 capacities $\times$ 20 origins $\times$ 8 arms, or 1{,}600 scored
runs. Gates checked before launch: the harness reproduces previously logged cells bit-exactly
(14 of 14); the shared teacher is identical across capacities to seven decimal places while
students are not; and every arm at a given (task, origin) is scored on an identical evaluation
set.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{The Cost of the Clock}

The Oracle-minus-Wait gap at $\Delta=7$\,d is $+0.00538$, $+0.00602$, $+0.00746$, $+0.00842$
and $+0.00598$ for $S_0$ to $S_4$, all with $p<0.001$ over 20 of 20 origins. At $\Delta=1$\,d it is
an order of magnitude smaller, from $+0.00067$ to $+0.00092$, though equally consistent (20 of 20,
$p<0.001$). Moving a head from a one-day to a seven-day clock therefore costs roughly $8\times$
more, which by \eqref{eq:coupling} is what consolidation imposes on a fast objective. Because the
$\Delta=1$\,d denominator is below $0.001$ $\NE$, we report absolute differences at that window and
treat recovery \emph{ratios} there as indicative only.

\subsection{Correction Attains Its Ceiling, and Then Falls Below It}
\label{sec:ceiling-attained}

\begin{figure}[t]
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figs/fig1_recovery.pdf}
\caption{The two ceilings. Correct (open circles) tracks the parameter-free prediction of
Proposition~\ref{prop:ceiling} (dashed) at the three smallest capacities and never crosses the
Oracle line at $1.0$. Distil (grey squares) is governed by neither bound: it exceeds the Oracle
throughout $\Delta{=}1$d and collapses at $S_4$ for $\Delta{=}7$d, where the teacher has almost no
headroom left}
\label{fig:recovery}
\end{figure}

For $\Delta=7$\,d the predicted ceiling is $0.868$, and Correct measures $0.883$, $0.874$, $0.849$,
$0.759$ and $0.625$ across $S_0$ to $S_4$. At the three smallest capacities the prediction is
accurate to $+0.015$, $+0.006$ and $-0.019$, from a formula with no free parameters fitted to
nothing. At $S_3$ and $S_4$ it over-predicts by $0.11$ and $0.24$.

The shortfall is misspecification, not estimation. Assumption \eqref{eq:a1} is false here in a way
we can measure: stratifying converted rows by one categorical field gives six strata of $23{,}790$
to $860{,}851$ conversions, across which the first-hour share of within-seven-day conversions
ranges from $0.453$ to $0.689$ (pooled $0.574$) and the median delay from $0.16$\,h to $1.73$\,h.
A model too small to resolve covariate-dependent delay cannot be harmed by a correction assuming it
away, so at $S_0$ to $S_2$ the only cost of censoring is the statistical one
Proposition~\ref{prop:ceiling} prices; as capacity grows the bias in $\hat F(e)$ becomes
expressible and recovery falls below the ceiling. Either way the practical reading is the same:
where the ceiling is attained nothing is left for a better correction to win, and where it is not,
the remedy is a covariate-conditional delay model rather than a tighter estimator.

\subsection{Distillation Is Not Bounded by the Oracle}
\label{sec:distil-escapes}

No correction-based strategy exceeds the Oracle at $\Delta=7$\,d: Correct is below it at every
capacity by $0.00063$ to $0.00224$ ($p<0.005$), Band by $0.0035$ to $0.0082$, and Reweight by
$0.0039$ to $0.0081$, all $p<0.001$. This is Proposition~\ref{prop:distil}'s first half, and
Correct's placement just under the bound is consistent with it being an efficient rather than a
wasteful estimator.

Distil behaves differently. At $\Delta=1$\,d it beats the infeasible Oracle at $S_0$ to $S_3$ by
$+0.00150$, $+0.00294$, $+0.00269$ and $+0.00397$, all $p<0.001$, and by $+0.00071$ ($p=0.06$) at
$S_4$. At $\Delta=7$\,d it is statistically indistinguishable from the Oracle at $S_0$ to $S_3$
($p=0.45$ to $0.73$) while every other strategy is significantly below it. Against Wait it gains
$+0.00217$ to $+0.00484$ at $\Delta=1$\,d and $+0.00419$ to $+0.00722$ at $\Delta=7$\,d over $S_0$
to $S_3$, all $p<0.001$ at 19 or 20 of 20 origins. Head to head with Correct it wins outright at
the short window, by $+0.00150$ to $+0.00411$ at $S_0$ to $S_3$ ($p<0.001$, 20 of 20 origins) and
$+0.00089$ at $S_4$ ($p=0.019$), and ties at the long one ($\pm0.0020$, $p=0.25$ to $0.80$). The
asymmetry follows from Sect.~\ref{sec:ceiling-attained}: at seven days Correct is near a ceiling
worth $0.868$ of a large penalty, whereas at one day the penalty is small and the teacher's
advantage is not.

One result cuts against Proposition~\ref{prop:distil} and we report rather than reconcile it. At
$\Delta=1$\,d, Reweight also exceeds the Oracle at $S_0$ ($+0.00199$) and $S_2$ ($+0.00041$),
$p<0.001$. The proposition is asymptotic, and Reweight trains on strictly less data under
non-uniform weights, which regularises; at a window whose entire penalty is under $0.001$ $\NE$ we
read these as finite-sample effects. They do not recur at $\Delta=7$\,d, where Reweight is below
the Oracle at all five capacities.

\subsection{The Gain Is Bought With Teacher Headroom}
\label{sec:headroom}

\begin{figure}[t]
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figs/fig2_penalty_decomp.pdf}
\caption{(a)~Distil's gain against teacher headroom $H$ (log axis), one point per capacity and
window. Gain is roughly flat while $H$ falls by $2.8\times$, then collapses at the single cell
where $H$ reaches $+0.0036$. (b)~Distil decomposed with the Distil-Stale control at
$\Delta{=}7$d. The fresher-covariate term is $47$ to $78\,\%$ of the gain}
\label{fig:decomp}
\end{figure}

Teacher headroom $H$ falls monotonically as the student grows, from $+0.0572$ at $S_0$ to
$+0.0036$ at $S_4$ for $\Delta=7$\,d, because the teacher is fixed while the student closes on it.
Distil's gain does not follow $H$ down. Across $S_1$ to $S_3$, where $H$ falls $2.8\times$ from
$0.0484$ to $0.0172$, the gain is essentially flat at $+0.00722$, $+0.00682$ and $+0.00709$: a
larger student extracts a larger fraction of a smaller advantage, with transfer efficiency
$\text{gain}/H$ rising from $14.9\,\%$ to $41.2\,\%$ (and $7.6\,\%$ to $31.0\,\%$ at
$\Delta=1$\,d).

The flatness ends abruptly. At $S_4$, $\Delta=7$\,d, headroom is $+0.0036$ and Distil collapses to
$-0.00015$ ($p=0.898$) --- the only non-positive cell in the grid, and the only one where the
Distil-Stale control is itself negative ($-0.00126$), meaning the teacher is actively degrading the
target. Distillation helps exactly while the teacher is meaningfully better than the student, which
is a statement about model size rather than about feedback delay. Our teacher, at $2{,}262\times$
the student's inference cost, is already spent against the largest student we tested; scaling the
teacher is therefore not an optional refinement of this result but the direction in which it
continues.

\subsection{What Distillation Is Actually Buying}
\label{sec:decomp}

Read alone, Distil's win over Wait looks like delay mitigation. The Distil-Stale control shows it
is only partly that. Capacity transfer accounts for $22$ to $53\,\%$ of the gain at $\Delta=7$\,d
across $S_0$ to $S_3$, and $71$ to $94\,\%$ at $\Delta=1$\,d (Fig.~\ref{fig:decomp}b). The
residual, the student's exposure to covariates Wait discarded and which carry no new label
information, is what is attributable to the delayed label; it is significant at every capacity
($p\le0.0013$, 19 or 20 of 20 origins) and worth $+0.0033$ to $+0.0045$ at $\Delta=7$\,d. Reporting
Distil against Wait alone would overstate delay mitigation by a factor of two to four at
$\Delta=1$\,d, and we would encourage this literature to treat a Distil-Stale-style control as
mandatory.

Band and Reweight do not repay their complexity: Band spans $-0.00215$ to $+0.00457$ with no
pattern in capacity and is significant in 5 of 10 cells, its slowest band still training on Wait's
window; Reweight stays within $-0.00055$ to $+0.00265$ of Wait everywhere.

\subsection{Calibration Transfers, Including Its Instability}
\label{sec:calib}

Because the served quantity is a price, calibration is not a diagnostic here but part of the
objective. Table~\ref{tab:calib} reports it two ways, and the two disagree.

On long-run bias Distil looks best of the feasible strategies and the teacher best of all. Per
origin the ranking inverts: Distil is the worst-calibrated arm, exceeding Wait's daily error by
$0.019$ ($p=5.7\times10^{-4}$, paired over 100 (capacity, origin) cells) and the Oracle's by
$0.020$. The mechanism is visible in the per-capacity values, which we give because they are
unusually clean: the teacher's daily error is $0.0501$ at every capacity by construction, and
Distil's is $0.0597$, $0.0508$, $0.0498$, $0.0519$ and $0.0501$ at $S_0$ to $S_4$, while Wait and
the Oracle sit at $0.028$ to $0.041$. The student does not estimate its calibration from labels; it
copies the teacher's, and it copies the day-to-day variance with it.

Calibration is thus a transferable property: a well-calibrated foundation model confers
calibration on every student it teaches at no serving cost, a second axis along which teacher
quality pays. But transfer is faithful in both directions, so where predictions are prices the
teacher must be calibrated \emph{per serving period}, not merely unbiased in the long run ---
accuracy alone is not a sufficient teacher-selection criterion. That this is operational rather
than an artefact of our setup is supported independently: the deployed system we take as
motivation reports stale supervision from teacher lag and adds a component realigning teacher
outputs to recent ground truth \cite{meta2025gem}.

\section{Limitations and Outlook}
\label{sec:limits}

The scope is deliberate and it bounds the conclusions. We motivate the work with multi-objective
consolidation but test $K=1$: \eqref{eq:coupling} is an argument, not a measurement, and we do not
show that a consolidated multi-head model behaves as the union of our single-objective cells.
Sparsity imbalance across objectives, the second obstruction we cite, cannot be studied on a corpus
with one conversion event, so it appears here only as motivation. And the extrapolation to
LLM-scale teachers is untested: we show the gain is governed by teacher headroom and that our
teacher's headroom is exhausted at $S_4$, not what three further orders of magnitude of teacher
would deliver. What the grid establishes is that the mechanism is real, that it is not bounded by
the quantity bounding every alternative here, that the alternative is already at its own ceiling,
and that calibration transfers along with accuracy.

On the measurements: one seed per origin, with the origin as the unit of replication ($n=20$);
seed sensitivity was characterised separately at a standard deviation of $0.0004$ to $0.0030$, an
order of magnitude below the $\Delta=7$\,d effects, but seeds are not averaged and the origins
share training data heavily, so the effective sample size is below twenty.
Proposition~\ref{prop:ceiling} assumes a $1/n$ excess-risk rate we do not verify directly;
Proposition~\ref{prop:distil} is asymptotic, with a finite-sample exception in
Sect.~\ref{sec:distil-escapes}. Distil's soft targets are largely teacher in-sample on
$\mathcal{M}$, and out-of-fold targets are untested. All results come from one public corpus with
one delay distribution, so Proposition~\ref{prop:ceiling}'s ceiling is corpus-specific even though
its formula is not.
